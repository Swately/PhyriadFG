# Learned post-process AA — is it the right next project?

**Run** `wf_ae1c045a-1aa`, 2026-09-07. Five code-and-web studies plus a judgement, answering two
operator questions: whether a learned AA should come BEFORE returning to img2img, and whether anything
already applies a DLAA-like filter across games.

> **The name is wrong and the document keeps correcting it.** DLAA runs INSIDE the renderer with
> jittered sub-pixel sampling. A capture-based overlay receives an ALREADY-RESOLVED frame — no jitter,
> no sub-pixel history, and you cannot un-resolve an image. What is on the table is a LEARNED TEMPORAL
> POST-PROCESS AA. Calling it DLAA would make a claim that is false in the first case a user tests:
> stand still and look at an edge.

# LEARNED POST‑PROCESS AA — ANSWER TO THE OPERATOR'S TWO QUESTIONS
**PhyriadFG, 2026‑09‑07. Four independent studies; this is the judgement, not the survey.**

---

## 1. THE ANSWER IN FIVE LINES

1. **Yes, before img2img** — but for one reason that outranks the four you were given: an AA target has a **reference**, and img2img never had one. E2B could not even run its own strobe test because no configuration produced style *and* structure; there was nothing to gate against. AA is gateable. That is the whole argument.
2. **It is not a wall like img2img, but it has a hard ceiling**, and the ceiling is set by *samples*, not by compute: with no jitter, a static camera on a static scene gives you exactly one sample per pixel forever. The filter is weakest exactly where a player stares at an aliased edge longest.
3. **The competitor is not "nothing", it is SMAA/CMAA2** — hand-written, ~0.05–0.30 ms, zero data, zero training, zero risk. If a learned head does not clearly beat that on your frames, the project has no reason to exist. Nobody in this repo has ever measured SMAA on a captured frame.
4. **The square is narrower than "nobody has done this."** Two capture-domain competitors shipped in the last eight weeks. The unfilled gap is precisely *"nobody has TRAINED a model for the capture domain"* — a real distinction, and a much weaker one than it sounded.
5. **Do not build a model yet.** Three to four days of instrument + baseline + one least‑squares oracle produce a number that decides the whole thing. If that number says CMAA2 is enough, ship CMAA2 in the `--afill` pass and you have answered the question for the price of a weekend.

## 2. QUESTION 2 — WHAT ALREADY EXISTS, AND IS THERE A GAP

Four families exist. Each fails one of your constraints, and the two newest fail it by less than you would like.

**(a) ReShade + SMAA / CMAA2 / FXAA / TSMAA** — genuinely system-wide, genuinely post-process on a finished frame, free, open. It gets there by injecting a renamed `dxgi.dll` / `d3d9.dll` / `opengl32.dll` into the game process (https://www.pcgamingwiki.com/wiki/ReShade). That is the one mechanism PhyriadFG exists to refuse. Riot's Vanguard auto-bans it; BattlEye's FAQ generally permits it "unless desired otherwise by the game developers." **TSMAA** is the closest hand-written analogue of your idea: SMAA plus previous-frame blending with *no motion vectors at all*, and the author's own claim is reduced shimmer "in exchange for" ghosting and occasional flicker (https://reshade.me/forum/shader-presentation/9443-tsmaa-subpixel-morphological-anti-aliasing-with-temporal-compositing). ReShade also has dense GPU optical-flow shaders (martymcmodding/ReShade-Optical-Flow, JakobPCoder/ReshadeMotionEstimation). Flow-guided post-process temporal AA already exists, classical and unlearned.

**(b) NVIDIA DLDSR** — the only *shipping learned AA on arbitrary games*, driver-level, no developer integration. It replaces DSR's 13‑tap Gaussian downsample with a network (https://www.hwcooling.net/en/nvidia-introduces-dldsr-dynamic-super-resolution-with-ai-scaling/). It solves the problem by taking the one route capture cannot take: **more information** — the game renders 1.78× or 2.25× the display pixels. Spatial only, no history, no flow. NVIDIA also ships driver-level AI filters (Freestyle, RTX HDR, RTX Dynamic Vibrance) across 1,200+ titles on Tensor Cores — proof that system-wide AI post-process is a shipped product — but has never shipped an AA filter that way. Note the validation of your never-hook rule: EAC blocked Freestyle, and NVIDIA disabled it in ARC Raiders at Embark's request (https://steamcommunity.com/app/1808500/discussions/0/690871474424254510/).

**(c) Magpie (Blinue, upstream)** — architecturally your sibling: Windows Graphics Capture / Desktop Duplication, no injection, "no reports of bans." 14,706 stars, 10.8 MB x64 zip, GPL‑3.0 (MEASURED via GitHub API 2026‑09‑07). **It ships FXAA_Medium/High/Ultra and SMAA_Low/Medium/High/Ultra** among 80+ effects (https://github.com/Blinue/Magpie/wiki/Built-in-effects). *Studies disagree here:* one study asserted Magpie ships no AA at all; two others quote the wiki's effect list directly. The wiki wins — **Magpie already applies SMAA to any window today**, which narrows your differentiator to *quality alone*. Its neural effects (Anime4K, CuNNy, FSRCNNX, RAVU) are all spatial upscalers.

**(d) The two things that shipped in the last eight weeks, and they are the ones that matter.**

- **SAOG0721/Magpie** (experimental fork): created 2026‑07‑14, 1,245 stars, v0.6.6‑experimental published 2026‑09‑06, `Magpie-Experimental-x64.zip` 487.4 MB with 10,766 downloads in roughly one day (MEASURED, GitHub API 2026‑09‑07). It runs **DLSS SR / FSR 2‑4 / XeSS SR on captured window frames driven by estimated optical flow, with a selectable 1× native-resolution mode** — which is functionally capture-domain DLAA. Its README states your exact constraint in its own words: "Magpie processes complete window images without access to the game engine's full native motion vectors, depth, exposure or separated UI. Estimated optical flow can assist some effects…" It also warns that "temporal effects may also produce ghosting" and that processing "can affect text and UI along with the scene." **Free negative result:** it shipped a 2.68 GB TensorRT monocular-depth estimator in v0.6.0/v0.6.1 (1–3 Sep) and **deleted it by v0.6.5 (5 Sep)**. Do not repeat that experiment.
- **DLSS5-Feeder** (https://github.com/jlrouzies-fr/DLSS5-Feeder) — feeds a synthetic DLAA contract to NVIDIA's DLSS 5 in games that ship no DLSS, taking depth from ReShade's depth-buffer detection and MVs from an optical-flow shader. It works *because injection hands it a real depth buffer.* Your own `DLSS5_RECON.md` already catalogued it (lines 181, 315‑316) and already declared DLAA "IMPOSSIBLE" under the capture constraint (line 100). Both are true and they do not conflict: remove injection and you remove depth.

**Verdict on the gap.** Split, and stated precisely:

> **No product does learned *temporal* post-process AA on arbitrary games from capture.** But a product *does* run learned temporal reconstruction on arbitrary captured frames with estimated flow, non-injecting, at 1× — and it shipped yesterday. The unoccupied square is not "capture-based temporal AA." It is **"a model trained on capture-domain data."** You would not be first; you would be the first to *train* rather than *integrate*.

**The third road, which you should weigh deliberately rather than by default:** the fork's weakest link is its flow estimate, and flow is your measured strength (GME affine + dissidence + backward pass + the M1 instrument). Feeding a vendor model with a better flow is a cheaper route to a better picture than training your own. It costs the single self-contained `.exe` (487.4 MB vs 10.8 MB) and adds a vendor dependency and an unresearched NGX licensing question. That trade is yours; make it on purpose.

## 3. THE FOUR REASONS, TESTED

**(1) "A ground truth exists." — SURVIVES, but only where you don't need it.**
This is still the strongest reason, and it is the real reason to reorder. Render at N×N, box-downsample, pair with 1× point-sampled: exact, deterministic, unlimited, no labelling, no human judgement. E2B failed because plain img2img "has no term that says keep this geometry" and there was nothing to optimize against.
The correction: **you can only supersample content you render.** You can supersample `marker_zoo`. You cannot supersample Honkai or BF6. The escapes are (a) DSR/DLDSR capture as the target — **which may not exist at all**, because DSR downsamples at scanout after DWM composition, so WGC may hand you the 3840×2160 *aliased* frame, i.e. the input, not the target (20-minute test, see §8); and (b) paused render-scale pairs, which are pixel-aligned by construction and **spatial only**. In motion, changing render scale changes the *content*, not just its aliasing: NVIDIA's own integration guide sets mip LOD bias to `log2(RenderRes/DisplayRes) − 1.0` (https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Streamline/blob/main/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS.md), and SSAO/SSR/shadow/bloom radii are specified in pixels. A net trained on that pair learns "render at 2×", not "anti-alias." One free correction nobody stated: use an **odd** supersample factor. At 2×2 the 1× pixel centre lands on the corner between four subsamples — a systematic **0.25 px** misregistration in every training pair. At 3×3, `(i+0.5)·3 = 3i+1.5` is exactly the centre subsample. Render scale 300% is right; 200% and DSR 4.00× are wrong parity.

**(2) "It is subtraction, not addition." — SURVIVES, and can be made structural.**
Make it structural rather than hoped-for: predict *kernels*, not colour (Bako et al., TOG 36(4) 2017, https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3072959.3073708). If the weights pass through a softmax so they sum to 1 over a support of **real neighbouring pixels**, the output is a convex combination of pixels that already exist. It cannot invent content, cannot shift hue, cannot create energy. E2B's failure is then impossible in principle, not merely unlikely.
The caveat that keeps it honest: on **sub-pixel thin geometry** — a wire, a fence, distant foliage — the information is destroyed, not compressed. Baker & Kanade named that class and called it hallucination, explicitly distinct from reconstruction (https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/baker_simon_2002_4/baker_simon_2002_4.pdf). A convex combination can only blur there; a free net would invent, and per-frame invention *is* temporal instability. And the HUD problem returns unchanged: E2B measured that at the one usable setting "the only visible change was the menu text turning to gibberish. Capture is post-composite and there is no UI mask."

**(3) "The cost class is already measured free." — SURVIVES WITH THREE MATERIAL CORRECTIONS.**
The headline holds: **0.3762 ms MEASURED** idle for a 1-layer 64-wide cooperative-matrix head at 1080p, **0.3351 ms (0.89×) MEASURED** under a 100% saturating competitor (E0, RTX 4090, driver 610.88, device timestamps, best of 25–30). Against **~95 ms MEASURED** per SD‑Turbo step at 512×512 on stable-diffusion.cpp/Vulkan (E2A). That is ~253–300× per frame (ESTIMATE — different resolutions, order-of-magnitude claim only).
Correction A: **E0's 0.4 ms cliff is a property of the whole submit, and your tick is already past it** — `iter_ms` 2.73 (flow-scale 1) / 0.94 (flow-scale 2), MEASURED on HSR 2026‑06‑22. An AA pass riding the existing `--afill` shape is a *marginal* cost on an already-penalised submit, not a fresh sub‑0.4 ms one.
Correction B: **every filter cost in this repo is priced per SOURCE frame at 30/s; AA runs per PRESENTED frame at 240/s — 8× the duty.** Unless you put it before the warp, in which case all 8 presents inherit it at 1/8 the cost but the warp's bilinear resample then works on already-AA'd edges. **Nobody has measured which loses less.**
Correction C: "free" is conditional on the 30 fps cap, and **the cap is not free.** `SATURATION_PLAN.md` §8.2, live uncapped BF6 with you playing, defaults, no filter: multiplier **0.84×**, present 89.4/s from 106.4 captured, latency 82.8 ms. The generator is a net loss there before anything is added. E1c says it plainly: the filter is free *given* that cap; the cap is not free.

**(4) "It needs no model licence and no model pack." — SURVIVES INTACT. This is the cleanest of the four.**
2,739 parameters = 5.5 KB FP16 embeds as a `constexpr` array exactly the way `CMakeLists.txt:48-65` already embeds every SPIR-V blob. **The single self-contained .exe survives**, which the stylizer route killed. Against SD‑Turbo's 2.58 GB FP16 (1.90 GB without the text encoder) plus the Stability AI Community License §4(a) obligation to ship a Notice file and display "Powered by Stability AI."

**Score: 2 survive intact, 1 survives with three corrections that change the engineering, 1 survives only in a reduced form.** The reordering still holds — but on reason (1) restated as *"AA is gateable and img2img was not"*, not on cost.

## 4. THE HARD PART, AT FULL FORCE

**What is genuinely recoverable.**
With DLAA off and render scale 1.0 your frames carry 1‑SPP geometric silhouette aliasing: a hard staircase on a long, high-contrast edge. The sub-pixel edge position and slope are **partially encoded in the run-length of that staircase**, and that is real information — it is exactly the cue MLAA/SMAA/CMAA2 decode. SMAA's own contribution over MLAA is described as "local contrast analysis for more reliable edge detection" and better "reconstruction of silhouettes" (https://graphics.unizar.es/papers/SMAA-Enhanced-Subpixel-Morphological-Antialiasing.pdf). For that class, post-process AA is not an approximation of the right answer. One structural advantage over the video-restoration literature: your capture is **uncompressed RGBA8 straight from the compositor** — no codec, no chroma subsampling, no block artifact.

**What motion buys, and what it costs.**
Camera motion genuinely supplies the sub-pixel sampling diversity that jitter normally provides. That is the entire foundation of multi-frame super-resolution; Google ships it on the Pixel using nothing but hand tremor (Wronski et al., SIGGRAPH 2019, https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03277). **But it is gated on registration accuracy, and registration error dominates every other noise term.** Baker & Kanade put the practical effective magnification at **1.6×** under noise and inadequate registration; recovering half-pixel edge structure is effectively a 2× problem. A ~2× fusion wants registration around **0.10–0.25 px (ESTIMATE from the SR literature; no single citable threshold for this configuration)**.

Your measured numbers, all on a **synthetic** corpus with ideal high-contrast markers — which exists precisely because it is the only thing with ground truth:
- Raw flow endpoint error: **0.464 px mean / 0.180 px median** (0.164 px on the linear class) — MEASURED, `zoo_static`, 2 × 16-triple captures.
- Shipping default's low-phase warp: **1.668 px** at t<0.25 — MEASURED; **0.271 px** flat across phase with `--no-mv-guided` (the 3×3 vector-median consensus pass is the cause).
- M1 at 8× from a 30 fps source: **hud 0.206 px (r=1.00), circular 0.791 px (r=0.50)** — the only two classes DI‑3 permits a verdict on. The four that carry no verdict: linear 0.633 / accel 2.373 / crossing 1.830 / **fast 5.462 px**. Those are *unmeasured*, not measured-good.

**And the mechanism is mismatched to the phenomenon.** The flow is **one vector per 8×8 block — 240×135 at 1080p, one vector per 64 pixels** (FATAL assert at `src/flow/flow_init.cpp:48-53`). Aliasing lives on 1–2 px features. Bilinearly interpolating a block field across a hard motion discontinuity gives a smoothly-varying flow that is wrong on **both** sides over an 8–16 px band — precisely at silhouettes, precisely where AA matters.

**Three things no amount of learning recovers:**
- **Sub-pixel features that missed the sample.** Gone in principle.
- **Shading aliasing.** A specular sparkle on a normal-mapped surface is an isolated bright pixel with no edge structure, indistinguishable from legitimate bright detail. Intel says of MSAA what applies with more force here: it "does not help with aliasing issues that arise during pixel shading."
- **The static case.** No jitter means a still camera samples the same sub-pixel phase forever. The temporal half degenerates to a spatial blur exactly when aliasing is most visible. **This is the inverse of TAA's profile** — TAA is rock-stable static and smears in motion; yours is empty static and works in motion. It must never be called "our own DLAA": the claim would be false in the case a user tests first (stand still, look at an edge).

**The realistic ceiling against SMAA.**
SMAA 1× at 1080p costs **0.30 ms on a GTX 1080 / 0.23 ms on Vega 64**; CMAA2 **0.15 / 0.10 ms**; FXAA **0.11 / 0.09 ms** (MEASURED, Intel's published table, https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/conservative-morphological-anti-aliasing-20.html). Scaled to this 4090: **~0.05–0.10 ms (ESTIMATE, 3–6× generational factor, bandwidth-bound pass, unmeasured on this rig)**. Zero data, zero training, zero risk, GPL source already written.

The honest anchor for what you'd land on is **TSMAA** — SMAA plus previous-frame blending, whose author reports less shimmer *in exchange for* ghosting and occasional flicker. With a real flow you should beat TSMAA. So the ceiling is:

> **Spatially SMAA-class with fewer pattern mistakes; temporally, reduced edge crawl while the camera moves and nothing when it does not; ghosting risk added. (ESTIMATE.)**

That is a genuine improvement. **It is not DLAA and it is not close.** And stated plainly, as you asked: **if a learned head cannot clearly beat SMAA — measured on your frames, outside the run-to-run spread — this project has no reason to exist.** The correct outcome then is to port CMAA2 into the `--afill` pass and stop, which is itself a real product: SMAA-class AA in games where injection is forbidden.

## 5. TRAINING — CAN ONE PERSON DO IT? HOURS, NOT ADJECTIVES

**Data volume is not the problem, and this is the encouraging half.** DnCNN‑S (17 layers, 64 wide, ~556K params) trains on 400 images → 204,800 patches of 40×40 for 50 epochs, and the paper states outright that a larger set "can only bring negligible improvements." That is **327.7 million pixels = 158 1080p-frame-equivalents ≈ 2.6 GB paired**. QRISP's *entire* dataset is 8,760 frames from 13 scenes; its smallest variant is **16 channels, one conv layer, 1.08 ms at 1080p on a 3090** (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.01483). SRPO does SR on *rasterized aliased* images with **8,434 parameters** (https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04198). You are not priced out of the model or the corpus.

**The temporal corpus is 200× worse.** The unit is sequences, not images (ARM NSS: 540p 1spp paired with 1080p 16spp, ~100-frame sequences). 30,000 paired 1080p RGBA8 frames = **~498 GB raw / 125–200 GB as PNG**. The dataloader, not the GPU, becomes the bottleneck.

**Generation cost.** `marker_zoo.py` measured at **26.9 ms/frame** (120 frames in 3.223 s, 1280×720, numpy CPU, this rig, 2026‑09‑07). At 1080p with 3×3 supersampling (20.25× the pixels): **~0.55 s/frame ESTIMATE → 10,000 paired frames in ~1.5 h of CPU.** Hand-harvested paused pairs: **30–60 s each → 480–960 in an 8-hour day (ESTIMATE)**, perfectly aligned, spatial only.

**Training cost.** DnCNN‑S: 6 h published on a Titan X (~6.7 TFLOPS FP32). This 4090 is 82.6 TFLOPS FP32 → **0.5–1 h per run (ESTIMATE**, discounted because small nets are launch- and dataloader-bound). That is the cheap part. The real figure is **20–40 runs of loss ablation, temporal-weight sweeps and architecture search = 1–3 weeks of wall clock (ESTIMATE)**, interleaved with your own use of the machine.

**The unpriced item, and it is the largest single risk in the plan — larger than the data.** `python -m pip list` on this rig, 2026‑09‑07 (MEASURED): `git-filter-repo, mpmath, numpy 2.5.2, pillow 12.3.0, pip, sympy`. **No PyTorch, no CUDA toolkit** (~3 GB install). And **nothing in this repo has ever exported trained weights to SPIR-V.** A self-contained `.exe` with no runtime dependency needs that path to exist. It is unbuilt, unestimated, and it must be proved on throwaway weights *before* any real training happens (see §8, day 3).

**Answer:** yes for a spatial head — days of data, hours per run, weeks of search. Conditional for a temporal head — the corpus is 200× larger, requires scene determinism only replays/benchmarks/cutscenes provide, and the domain gap between engine-rendered pairs and WGC-captured commercial frames is unbounded and unmeasurable in advance.

## 6. THE ARCHITECTURE

**KPN‑TAA** — one compute shader, one dispatch, riding the existing `--afill` pass shape (in-place per-pixel dispatch on `wapOutA` inside `cmdBridge`, same submit, no host bounce, byte-identical when off).

Per pixel, 8×8 workgroup with a 10×10 shared tile (100 loads / 64 pixels = 1.56 loads/px):
1. Gather the current 3×3 luma patch + centre RGB in YCoCg.
2. Reproject the previous **output** by the **backward** MV field (binding 5) — bilinear.
3. **Variance-clip** that history to μ ± γσ of the same 3×3 neighbourhood (Salvi 2016; Lottes 2011 / Karis 2014 for the AABB form; surveyed in k‑DOP Clipping, SIGGRAPH Asia 2024 TC, https://dl.acm.org/doi/fullHtml/10.1145/3681758.3697996). ~16–30 ALU ops over samples already in shared memory: **<0.01 ms ESTIMATE**.
4. MLP **32 → 32 → 32 → 19**, ReLU. Inputs: 9 current luma (mean-centred, local-σ-normalised), 3 current YCoCg, 9 clipped-history luma, 3 history YCoCg, `d_pixel` (per-pixel source disagreement, already computed), persistence (binding 8), |mv|, sad_best/sad_zero.
5. Outputs 9 spatial + 9 history kernel weights + 1 alpha through a **joint softmax**. Output = convex combination of real pixels.

**2,739 parameters. 5.5 KB FP16. 2,656 MAC/px = 11.0 GFLOP/frame. ESTIMATE 0.25–0.40 ms at 1080p** (scaled from E0's measured 0.3762 ms at ~4,900 MAC/px; the bracket adds the tile gather, a dependent history fetch, YCoCg, the clip and an 18-tap sum, which the probe does not do). **This number has never been measured and the whole recommendation rests on it.**

**No cooperative matrix needed** — Vulkan's own proposal says coopmat is the wrong primitive for per-invocation networks ("cooperative matrices are by nature shared across many threads"). `VK_NV_cooperative_vector rev 4` is confirmed present on this exact 4090 (first-hand probe, `FILTER_SCOPE_30FPS.md:520`) if it helps later, but keep plain FP16 FMA with weights as compile-time constants as the portable path. The catastrophic 240.5 ms software figure in the literature is against a shader reading float32 weights from VRAM per invocation; this net does not do that.

**Input rulings.**
- **NO Sobel (binding 11).** `gx`/`gy` are fixed linear combinations of the 3×3 luma patch the net already receives, so the first linear layer spans them at zero cost, and `|gx|+|gy|` is four ReLU units. It costs 8.29 MB of PCIe per real frame (~0.99 GB/s at 120 fps, half of it padding) plus an iGPU dependency, to supply information the net already has. **This reverses the stylizer ruling, correctly:** a 3D LUT has no neighbourhood, so Sobel was genuinely new information *there*.
- **NO edge class** — a threshold at a hand-picked global constant is exactly what a learned nonlinearity replaces.
- **NO forward MV** — a history reprojection needs the backward field.
- **YES `d_pixel`/SAD** (per-pixel, already computed, already used as `garbage_w`), **persistence**, **|mv|**.

**Two corrections to the premise, verified first-hand this session:**
- `shaders/fg_core.comp` — the shipping default kernel since R7(a) — declares bindings 0–8, 10, 12, 15. Its own header states: *"Bindings this kernel does not read (9 mass counter, 11 contour field, 13 prev-output) are not declared here."* **The contour field and the history slot are not inputs to the default kernel.**
- `src/present/present.cpp:1310` gates the output→history copy on `cfg.ts_smooth > 0.0f && wapPrevOutA.img`, and `src/control/cli.hpp:176` initialises `ts_smooth = 0.0f`. **`wapPrevOutA` is not even allocated by default.** A head reading binding 13 as "history" would silently get the current frame — a gate-passing failure.

So: "the inputs are already computed" is **half true**. Colour, bidirectional flow, SAD, dissidence and persistence are there. **The history buffer does not exist and must be built**: allocate `wapPrevOutA` unconditionally (8.29 MB), copy every tick, and change the fetch from same-uv (`wap_warp.comp:1259` — *"same uv; no motion-comp → it trails"*) to a backward-MV reprojection. That is the new plumbing, and it is small but non-zero.

## 7. THE GATE — **A1**, in this project's idiom

Four **conjunctive** clauses, so no single metric can be gamed. Clause (b) exists because you already own the precedent.

**Why the standard metrics lie here, all in the same direction:** PSNR, SSIM and warping error **all reward blur**. Against a supersampled reference, a Gaussian blur scores *better* than the aliased input, because the reference is smoother than the input. Correct AA and incorrect blur both move the image toward the reference on an L2/structural measure and neither metric separates them. Your own repo records the case: **warping error 18.34 → 12.96 while Sobel edge magnitude fell 60%** — "a metric win that was a quality loss." *(That figure is quoted from `DLSS5_RECON.md`, attributed to arXiv 2606.05981; I did not independently verify the paper exists. The rule is derivable without it.)*

- **A1‑a (it antialiases).** On the synthetic `edges` class, mean FLIP against the `--ss 4` reference improves versus OFF, with per-class run-to-run r ≥ 0.5.
- **A1‑b (IT DID NOT BLUR — the anti-lie clause).** On the same frames, mean 10–90% edge transition width must not exceed the reference's by more than the threshold, **and** mean Sobel magnitude must not fall below the reference's by more than the threshold. **If either fails, A1‑a is void regardless of margin.** Blur and correct AA are indistinguishable to PSNR/SSIM/E_warp and trivially distinguishable to *edge-transition width paired with sub-pixel edge centroid measured normal to a known analytic edge* — which is available only because `marker_zoo` has analytic truth.
- **A1‑c (no temporal artifacts).** Static/`hud` arm: per-pixel σ over 120 frames must not increase; mean FLIP between successive frames must not increase (the k‑DOP protocol: no animation, so any inter-frame difference is the algorithm's own instability). `crossing` arm: `marker_extract`'s **already-computed-and-discarded `n_peaks ≥ 2` ghost flag** must not increase — a second NCC peak at a marker's previous position *is* a ghost, and turning that recorded column into a scored one is nearly free. And M1 must not regress: `circular` 0.791 px and `hud` 0.206 px within the measured run-to-run spread.
- **A1‑d (it fits).** Device-timestamp cost of the AA dispatch at 1080p ≤ **0.35 ms** (inside E0's measured free band: free at 0.3762, penalised at 0.7222), measured **at 240 presents/s, not 30**, with FG holding `frz 0.0/s`, `uniq == fresh == presented`, `iter worst` inside 4.167 ms.

**Thresholds are set BEFORE any model exists**, at 3× the measured OFF-baseline run-to-run spread, and published in the evidence doc. P‑014 is the reason: at n=2 the within-side spread came out exactly zero, so a one-present difference over 10,788 read as significant. **n=2 with r reported (r < 0.5 = UNRELIABLE = no verdict); n=4 per side for any A/B, alternated, second block reversed.**

**Three mandatory baselines:** (1) OFF; (2) **a hand-written CMAA2/SMAA-class compute pass — from day one**; (3) `--ts-smooth 0.5`, the naive temporal baseline that already ships and whose own help text records its failure ("smear medido a fuente rapida").

**STOP RULES.**
- **STOP 1** — baseline (2) passes A1‑a and A1‑b and nothing learned beats it outside the run-to-run spread → **ship the hand-written pass, train nothing.**
- **STOP 2** — no configuration passes A1‑a and A1‑b *together* → structurally identical to E2B ("there is no strength that gives both"). Record it as a structural fact; do not tune around it.
- **STOP 3** — A1‑d fails at one layer → the answer is not a bigger head; the design is dead at this rig's contention law.
- **STOP 4** — synthetic arm passes, real arm's no-reference probes contradict it → the model learned `marker_zoo`, not games. No amount of training on `marker_zoo` fixes that.

## 8. STAGE 1 — THE CHEAPEST EXPERIMENT THAT PRODUCES A NUMBER

**Four days. No model. No PyTorch until day 3. Stop at the first red.**

**Day 0 (20 minutes) — GATE 0, the DSR capture test.** Enable DSR 4×, run `--qdump`, read the dumped frame's dimensions from the manifest. If it dumps **3840×2160**, DSR/DLDSR hands you the *input*, not the target, and that ground-truth route is dead — fall back to paused render-scale pairs (spatial only) and to `marker_zoo` (synthetic only). *This should have been run before any of this was discussed.*

**Day 1 — the corpus.** Extend `marker_zoo.py` with `--alias`, `--ss N` (use **3**, odd parity) and an `edges` class (long bars at 1°, 5°, 15°, 45° × 3 contrasts — the existing 6/12/24 px classes were chosen for NCC distinctness, not edge geometry; at 1° the staircase period is analytically `1/tan θ` px). **The trap, documented in the file and worth a day if rediscovered:** `splat()`'s antialiasing "is not cosmetic here" — a grid-snapped edge makes consecutive frames byte-identical, the capture dedup collapses them, and the source lies about its own rate. `--alias` must alias *spatially* while still moving *sub-pixel temporally*. **See the gate red first:** assert `--alias` FAILS the edge-width test against its own `--ss 3` reference and `--ss 3` passes.

**Day 2 — `tools/aa_truth/aa_report.py`.** Edge-geometry probe + shimmer probe, reusing `marker_extract`'s barcode decode and parabolic sub-pixel verbatim (measured capture-path floor 0.116–0.119 px against its own 0.25 px bar) and `motion_report`'s DI‑3 r machinery verbatim.

**Day 3 — THE NUMBER, and this is the kill test.** Capture a deterministic sequence at 1× and at the reference. Fit the **best possible 3×3 + reprojected-history kernel per pixel by least squares against the reference** — the *upper bound of the entire architecture class*, not a bad implementation of it. Report, twice each:

> **PSNR/SSIM/FLIP + edge-width + Sobel-magnitude of: (a) the least-squares oracle, (b) CMAA2, (c) the source. The number that decides the project is the oracle's margin over CMAA2, expressed in units of the measured run-to-run spread.**

**STOP RULE:** if the oracle does not beat CMAA2 by a margin you can *see* in a flip test and that exceeds 3× the spread, **no network can either** — because the oracle is the ceiling of the class. Ship CMAA2 in the `--afill` pass, write the evidence doc, and the question is answered for four days.

**Day 3b, in parallel (0.5 day, no C++) — the premise probe.** `--qdump` a stationary camera, a slow pan and a fast pan. Per pixel, measure the variance of the sequence after reprojection by the existing flow. If the static case sits at or below the capture noise floor, **write in the README that this filter does nothing when you stand still** and price the feature accordingly.

**Only if all of that passes: Day 4+ — prove the toolchain before proving the model.** Install PyTorch, train a deliberately tiny 4-layer 16-channel net on the synthetic corpus, and push it through **weights → ONNX → SPIR-V → a shader in the `--afill` dispatch**. Its job is not quality; it is to prove the export path exists on content whose ground truth is analytic, for the price of a day. That is the M4 oracle's gate shape, applied to the toolchain instead of the algorithm.

## 9. WHAT THIS MEANS FOR img2img

**PARKED, WAITING ON TWO SPECIFIC THINGS.** Not dead, and not "later" — named unblocks, so it can be revived on evidence rather than on mood.

E2B's wall is not "the settings were wrong." It is that **plain img2img has no term that says keep this geometry**, and the control turned out to be an integer step count with no dial between preserve and replace. Two things unblock it, and neither is a tuning exercise:

1. **A model with a structure-conditioning term.** ControlNet-class conditioning (canny/depth/edge) or a distilled one-step *instruction-edit* model — something whose objective contains the constraint E2B found missing. Reviving img2img with an unconditioned generator is repeating a measured experiment.
2. **A runtime that reaches the TensorRT class.** ~95 ms/step MEASURED on the Vulkan runtime you actually have, against the 10.65 ms TensorRT literature anchor that was never reproduced on this rig — **~9×**, and the single largest un-transferred number in the whole filter budget. At 95 ms/step there is no product regardless of quality.

**A third blocker is shared with AA and is the reason to do AA first: the UI mask.** E2B's very first usable setting turned menu text to gibberish, and the SAOG0721 fork independently warns that processing "can affect text and UI along with the scene." Neither path ships without one, and neither can obtain it from outside the process. The obvious heuristic — temporal stability of static screen regions — is *exactly* what the AA work's persistence/`d_pixel`/static-arm instrumentation is built on. **The AA project builds the thing img2img is blocked on**, plus the paired-data discipline and the gate machinery.

So the order is correct, and for a reason better than "AA is cheaper": **AA is gateable, img2img is not yet, and AA produces the UI mask and the gate discipline that img2img will need on the day its two unblocks land.**

## APPENDIX — WHERE THE STUDIES DISAGREE

1. **Does Magpie ship AA?** Two studies say yes (FXAA_Medium/High/Ultra, SMAA_Low–Ultra, quoting the built-in-effects wiki); one says no. **Resolved: yes** — the wiki names them. Your differentiator narrows to quality.
2. **Is the square empty?** One study: "the square is empty." Another: SAOG0721/Magpie shipped a capture-based, non-injecting, 1×-native learned temporal reconstruction **55 days ago**. **Resolved: the second is right**; the empty square is specifically *trained-on-capture-domain-data*.
3. **Which competitor is closest?** One says SAOG0721/Magpie (capture-based, non-injecting); another says DLSS5‑Feeder (injecting). **Both are right about different constraints** — Feeder is closer in *capability* and fails your hard constraint; the fork is closer in *architecture* and does not.
4. **Do bindings 11/13 already give you the inputs?** One study lists them as an existing strength; two say 13 binds the current frame unless `--ts-smooth > 0`; one says neither is declared in the default kernel. **Resolved first-hand this session: neither is declared in `fg_core.comp`, and `wapPrevOutA` is not allocated at the default `ts_smooth = 0.0f`.** The history buffer is new work.
5. **Is Sobel (binding 11) worth feeding the net?** One study lists it as an asset; another rules it out as redundant with the 3×3 patch. **Resolved: rule it out for a net with a neighbourhood** — and note the earlier stylizer ruling was right *for a LUT*, which has none.
6. **Per-tick headroom at 240 Hz.** `E1D §1` reads warp 4.04 / iter 4.17 / worst 4.41 ms against a 4.167 ms tick — essentially none. `E1D §3` reads iter 2.39 / worst 2.92 ms with a 9.8 ms probe co-resident. `E1D §2` says "+9 W for +20 points of utilization… latency-bound, not compute-bound." **Both readings are defensible from the record; they imply anywhere from ~0 to ~1.7 ms of room. This is the single most decision-relevant unmeasured quantity in the plan, it is a same-day measurement, and A1‑d is the run that settles it.**
7. **Where does the AA pass go?** Before the warp (30/s, 8× cheaper, but the warp then bilinearly resamples AA'd edges at a fractional offset — and the shipping default has a measured 1.668 px low-phase bias at that hook) or after (240/s, 8× the duty). **Unresolved by any measurement. Decide it with a number, not an argument.**

- **WHY the training gap is unfilled, and it is a physics reason before it is a market reason: with no jitter, a STATIC camera on a STATIC scene provides exactly one sample per pixel forever, no matter how many frames you accumulate. ### How much anti-aliasing is recoverable from an already-resolved captured frame — scoping a learned temporal post-process AA for PhyriadFG, for one solo developer, against SMAA as the free baseline

- **The framing correction is right and must be held: what is on the table is not DLAA. DLAA/TAA/DLSS/FSR2/XeSS all require inputs that do not exist in a composited frame — jittered colour, per-frame sub-pixel jitter offset, depth, and engine-exact per-pixel motion vectors. This is not a soft requirement; PhyriadFG's own reconnaissance already established it from primary vendor docs.**
  - Streamline/DLSS programming guide: 'DLSS requires depth, motion vectors, render-res input color and final-res output color buffers'; camera matrices 'should not contain any jitter offsets'; only exposure is optional. GPUOpen FSR SDK: required resources are render-res colour, 32-bit depth, 2D render-res motion vectors, and 'the subpixel jitter offset applied to the camera'. Intel XeSS: 'Jitter, Input color, Dilated high-res motion vectors ... plus Depth'. The recon's own conclusion: 'you cannot feed a zeroed depth buffer and a self-estimated MV field to DLSS SR and get anything but garbage ... Do not spend a week finding this out empirically.'
  - source: F:\Phyriad\projects\PhyriadFG\docs\research\DLSS5_RECON.md lines 41-42, 100, 383-384, 393, 449; https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/super-resolution-spatial/
- **The industry itself has stated that a purely spatial method cannot fix an aliased input — and its fix was to go back into the renderer, not to get smarter about the image. FSR 1.0 required an already anti-aliased source; the headline change in FSR 2.0 was producing anti-aliased output from aliased input, achieved by adding jitter + motion vectors + depth. That is the cleanest available external evidence on the ceiling of image-only AA.**
  - AMD GPUOpen: FSR 1.0 'requires a high quality anti-aliased source image, which is not always available'; 'FSR 1.0 is not an anti-aliasing solution itself'. FSR 2.0 announcement frames producing an anti-aliased result from aliased input as the advance, and its input contract is render-res depth + MVs + camera jitter offset.
  - source: https://gpuopen.com/news/fsr2-announce/ and https://gpuopen.com/fidelityfx-superresolution/
- **What is destroyed before capture, specifically: (1) the per-frame sub-pixel jitter offset — a scalar pair consumed by the rasterizer, gone with no trace in the pixels; (2) unresolved MSAA/SSAA sub-samples — the resolve is a many-to-one box filter, irreversible; (3) depth, used by TAA for reprojection and disocclusion; (4) engine-exact per-pixel motion vectors; (5) linear-space radiance — the frame is post-tonemap, 8-bit sRGB, so the true pre-tonemap contrast ratio across a high-dynamic-range edge is unrecoverable and a correct coverage blend (a linear-space average) can only be approximated; (6) any semantic separation of HUD from scene — the frame is post-composite.**
  - TAA implementations jitter the projection matrix with a Halton(2,3) sequence of length 16 and blend with an EMA factor ~0.05, then rectify with variance-box neighbourhood clamping in YCoCg. None of the jitter schedule, the pre-resolve samples, or the depth survives into the swapchain image the compositor hands to Windows Graphics Capture. PhyriadFG's own docs already record the HUD half: 'there is no semantic separation available: a style filter restyles the HUD along with the scene.'
  - source: http://behindthepixels.io/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf (Yang et al., A Survey of Temporal Antialiasing Techniques, EG 2020); https://interplayoflight.wordpress.com/2020/05/30/a-survey-of-temporal-antialiasing-techniques-presentation-notes/; F:\Phyriad\projects\PhyriadFG\docs\research\FILTER_PLAN.md §
- **One favourable structural fact that separates this from video restoration: PhyriadFG's capture path is uncompressed RGBA8 straight from the compositor. There is no codec, no chroma subsampling, no block artifact. Whatever aliasing is present is the game's own, not a transport artifact. That is a materially easier starting image than the video-restoration literature assumes.**
  - The pipeline's own resource accounting works in RGBA8 full-res images throughout (u_prev_real / u_cur_real RGBA8, 8,294,400 B at 1080p) with the capture arriving via WGC/Desktop Duplication and no encode stage anywhere in the chain.
  - source: F:\Phyriad\projects\PhyriadFG\docs\planning\aap\AT1_VERDICT.md lines 16-17; docs\research\FILTER_PLAN.md line 37
- **There IS a genuinely recoverable component, and it is the one the operator's setup produces most of. With DLAA off and render scale 1.0, the dominant artifact is 1-SPP geometric silhouette aliasing: a hard step pattern on a long, high-contrast edge. The sub-pixel edge position and slope are partially encoded in the run-length of that staircase, which is exactly the cue MLAA/SMAA/CMAA2 decode. For long straight high-contrast edges, post-process AA is not an approximation of the right answer — it computes coverage from a cue that really is present in the resolved image.**
  - SMAA's stated contribution over MLAA is 'local contrast analysis for more reliable edge detection' and 'a simple and effective way to handle sharp geometric features and diagonal lines, allowing for better reconstruction of silhouettes' — i.e. the family works by re-deriving silhouette geometry from the pattern, and successive papers improved the decoding rather than adding data.
  - source: https://graphics.unizar.es/papers/SMAA-Enhanced-Subpixel-Morphological-Antialiasing.pdf (Jimenez et al., SMAA, CGF 2012)
- **What is NOT recoverable by any amount of learning from a single resolved frame: sub-pixel features. A wire, a fence, a foliage twig thinner than a pixel either hit the single sample or it did not. If it missed, the pixel contains zero information about it, and the best a network can do is hallucinate a plausible one — which in a game is per-frame temporal instability by construction. Baker & Kanade named this class exactly and called it hallucination/recogstruction, distinguishing it from reconstruction.**
  - Baker & Kanade show the reconstruction constraints 'provide less and less useful information as the magnification factor increases' and propose a separate class of algorithm that 'recognize[s] local features in the low resolution images and then enhance[s] their resolution, which they call a hallucination or recogstruction algorithm' — an explicit acknowledgement that beyond the reconstruction limit you are inventing, not recovering.
  - source: https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/baker_simon_2002_4/baker_simon_2002_4.pdf (Limits on Super-Resolution and How to Break Them, PAMI 2002)
- **Also not recoverable: shading aliasing. A specular highlight aliasing on a normal-mapped surface produces an isolated bright pixel with no edge structure, indistinguishable from a legitimate bright detail. Intel says of MSAA what applies with more force to any post-process method: it 'does not help with aliasing issues that arise during pixel shading'. Neither SMAA nor a learned image-only filter has any principled way to tell the two apart.**
  - Intel's CMAA2 article states the pixel-shading limitation directly and additionally acknowledges CMAA2 'cannot address temporal or subpixel artifacts without integration' and 'does not handle diagonal aliasing patterns like SMAA does'.
  - source: https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/conservative-morphological-anti-aliasing-20.html
- **The classical baseline is not merely free, it is inside PhyriadFG's own measured free tier by a comfortable margin. Intel's measurements put SMAA at 0.30 ms and CMAA2 at 0.15 ms at 1080p on a GTX 1080 — hardware roughly 4-6x slower than this 4090. Scaled, both land well under the 0.4 ms submit threshold at which E0's contention law starts charging 3-8x. A learned method must beat a competitor that costs approximately nothing and requires zero data, zero training and zero risk.**
  - Intel, 1080p added cost: CMAA2 0.15 ms (GTX 1080) / 0.10 ms (Vega 64); FXAA 0.11 / 0.09 ms; SMAA 0.30 / 0.23 ms. E0 measured on this 4090: a 0.3762 ms submit is free (0.89x under 100% contention), a 0.7222 ms submit pays 1.7-8x.

  - source: https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.04198
- **THE TEMPORAL CRUX, principle first: yes, motion in principle supplies the sampling diversity that jitter normally provides. This is the entire foundation of multi-frame super-resolution — sub-pixel-shifted views carry different aliasing, and fusing them on a finer grid recovers frequencies above the individual sampling limit. Wronski et al. harness natural hand tremor for exactly this, and their framing is that aliasing is the ally, not the enemy. Camera motion in a game is the direct analogue of hand tremor.**
  - Google/SIGGRAPH 2019: the algorithm 'harnesses natural hand tremor, typical in handheld photography, to acquire a burst of raw frames with small offsets'; 'the natural hand tremor of a smartphone user provides the very sub-pixel sampling necessary for resolution enhancement'; because each frame is a sub-pixel-shifted view, 'aliasing present in each of the images is slightly different'. The Pixel 3 Super Res Zoom product ships on this principle.
  - source: https://arxiv.org/abs/1905.03277; https://research.google/blog/see-better-and-further-with-super-res-zoom-on-the-pixel-3/
- **But the condition that makes it work is sub-pixel registration accuracy, and registration error is the dominant error term in the whole method — larger than blur noise or additive noise. Baker & Kanade put the practical effective magnification at ~1.6x in the presence of noise and inadequate registration, with the useful information from the reconstruction constraints decaying rapidly as magnification rises. Recovering half-pixel edge structure is effectively a 2x problem, i.e. already at or past that limit.**
  - 'In the case of presence of noise and inadequate registration accuracy, the practical limit for an effective magnification factor is 1.6'; noise at high frequencies not suppressed by the SR algorithm 'grow[s] quadratically with the SR magnification factor'. The multi-frame SR literature separately identifies registration noise (Type II error) as having the most significant impact on reconstruction quality among all noise sources.
  - source: https://www.ri.cmu.edu/pub_files/pub3/baker_simon_2002_4/baker_simon_2002_4.pdf

- **It only works while the camera moves, and that is not a caveat, it is half the use case. With a static camera every frame samples the same sub-pixel phase and there is nothing to fuse; the method degrades exactly to its spatial-only ceiling. This is why jitter is injected in the first place, and a capture-based overlay cannot inject jitter. The static case is also where aliasing is most visible, because there is no motion to mask it.**
  - TAA practitioner statement of the mechanism: 'without any motion, TAA is just sampling the same locations over and over and there's nothing to blend ... when the camera sits still, the image looks super aliased'; the fix is sub-pixel jittering of the projection matrix. Pure integer-pixel panning gives the same degenerate case. Yang et al. define TAA as temporally-amortized supersampling — amortized over samples the renderer chooses to place, which an overlay does not control.
  - source: https://alextardif.com/TAA.html; http://behindthepixels.io/assets/files/TemporalAA.pdf
- **TAA's own history-rectification step caps how much sub-pixel gain survives, and a capture-based method would pay the same cap. Neighbourhood variance clamping in YCoCg clamps the accumulated history back into the current frame's local colour range — it deliberately re-limits the detail it just collected, in exchange for not ghosting. Any flow-based accumulator PhyriadFG builds needs the same reject mechanism and inherits the same ceiling, plus a worse one, because its flow is estimated rather than exact.**
  - Yang et al. survey: 'To limit ghosting artifacts for disoccluded content, variance-box neighborhood clamping in the YCoCg color space is used'; blend factor ~0.05, Lanczos-3 reprojection kernel to limit resampling blur. - **There is a second structural fact that must be stated before any data is generated: for an already-resolved frame, aliased -> anti-aliased is NOT A FUNCTION, so the net can only ever learn a prior, never a reconstruction.**
  - Many distinct sub-pixel geometries produce identical aliased pixels and resolve to different anti-aliased pixels. Where the prior is strong (long, high-contrast, near-axis-aligned silhouettes) the net will do well. Where the information is genuinely destroyed — sub-pixel thin geometry: wires, fences, hair, distant foliage, specular sparkle on normal-mapped surfaces — the net will invent, and inventing is exactly what E2B condemned in the img2img path. Aliasing also actively DEFEATS the flow stage that would supply the history: an aliased edge crawls, and an 8x8 block matcher tracks the crawl rather than the object. Nobody has measured this interaction in PhyriadFG.

- **Route 2's trap has a name in the literature, but the AA-specific version of the trap is WORSE and different from the SR version, and almost nobody states it correctly.**
  - In super-resolution the trap is the bicubic-downsampling assumption / degradation-model mismatch / domain gap: nets trained on bicubically downsampled pairs 'suffer severe performance drop in real applications' because the real kernel differs. It is the founding problem of blind and real-world SR and the reason the NTIRE 2020 Real-World SR challenge exists. The AA version is sharper: you CANNOT MANUFACTURE ALIASING BY LOW-PASS DOWNSAMPLING. A properly filtered downsample of a supersampled render IS the anti-aliased image — it is the TARGET, not the input. To make the input you must DECIMATE by point-sampling (take one subsample per output pixel, no prefilter), which is precisely what a 1x no-AA render does. So the route is not automatically wrong; it is wrong exactly and only where the game's shading is resolution-dependent.
  - source: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2005.01996 (NTIRE 2020 Challenge on Real-World Image Super-Resolution) ; https://www4.comp.polyu.edu.hk/~cslzhang/paper/ICCV-RealSR.pdf ; https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-024-60157-9
- **Route 2 gets GEOMETRIC edge aliasing right and gets SHADER aliasing wrong — and shader aliasing is the thing people actually buy DLAA for.**
  - Point-sampling an NxN supersample reproduces silhouette/geometric aliasing exactly (rasterisation coverage at the sampled point is identical). What it misrepresents: (a) texture aliasing, because the 2x/3x render selects a finer mip — NVIDIA's own bias formula quantifies this at exactly log2(render/display) mip levels; (b) specular and normal-map shimmer, because shading is evaluated per rendered pixel and the supersampled pass evaluates it N^2 times; (c) dithered/stochastic transparency (foliage, hair); (d) the post chain — bloom/DoF/motion blur kernels are in pixels; (e) film grain and any per-pixel noise; (f) the UI, which most engines composite at output resolution and never supersample. So a net trained this way learns a good edge model and a WRONG shimmer model. In modern deferred PBR renderers specular shimmer is the dominant complaint. The trap is therefore material, not academic.
  - source: https://github.com/NVIDIAGameWorks/Streamline/blob/main/docs/ProgrammingGuideDLSS.md ; derived reasoning about deferred-renderer post chains
- **A concrete, cheap correction to Route 2 that nobody in this conversation has stated: use an ODD supersample factor. 2x is the wrong parity and introduces a systematic 0.25 px offset.**
  - With 2x2 ordered-grid supersampling the 1x pixel centre at (i+0.5) maps to 2i+1 in the 2x grid, which sits on the CORNER between four subsamples — no 2x subsample coincides with the 1x sample point, so the point-sampled input is offset by half a 2x-pixel (0.25 px at 1x) in both axes from a true 1x render. With 3x3, (i+0.5)*3 = 3i+1.5, which is exactly the centre subsample's own centre. For a network whose entire job lives at the sub-pixel scale, a systematic 0.25 px shift in every training pair is not a rounding error — it is a learned constant misregistration. Render scale 300% is the right setting; render scale 200% and DSR 4.00x (which is 2x2) are the wrong ones.

- **The standard ghosting mitigations are essentially FREE here, because the data they need is already the net's input. Neighbourhood clamping (Lottes 2011, improved to YCoCg clipping by Karis 2014) clamps the reprojected history to the AABB of the current 3x3 neighbourhood; variance clipping (Salvi 2016) clips instead to mu +/- gamma*sigma of that same neighbourhood, giving a tighter box and a tunable ghosting/stability trade. THE ARCHITECTURE — "KPN-TAA", one compute shader, one dispatch, riding the existing --afill pass shape (in-place on wapOutA, same submit, byte-identical when off):

Per pixel, 8x8 workgroup with a 10x10 shared-memory tile (100 loads for 64 pixels = 1.56 loads/px):
 1. Gather the current frame's 3x3 luma patch + centre RGB in YCoCg.
 2. Reproject the previous OUTPUT by the BACKWARD MV field (binding 5) — bilinear fetch.
 3. Variance-clip that history to mu +/- gamma*sigma of the current 3x3 neighbourhood (Salvi 2016), gamma ~1.
 4. MLP, 32 -> 32 -> 32 -> 19, ReLU: 9 current luma (mean-centred, local-std-normalised), 3 current YCoCg, 9 clipped-history luma, 3 history YCoCg, d_pixel (per-pixel source disagreement, already computed in fg_core), persistence (binding 8), |mv|, sad_best/sad_zero, padding.
 5. Outputs 9 spatial kernel weights + 9 history weights + 1 temporal alpha through a JOINT SOFTMAX so they sum to 1. Output = convex combination of real pixels.

**Unknowns:**
- The per-tick GPU headroom at 240 Hz for a pass that runs on every PRESENTED frame. E1d §1 (warp 4.04 ms of a 4.167 ms tick) and E1d §3 (iter 2.39/worst 2.92 with a 9.8 ms probe co-resident) cannot both be right about it, and every filter figure in the repo is priced at 30/s rather than 240/s. This is the single most decision-relevant unmeasured quantity and it is a same-day measurement.
- Whether an aliased-and-still-sub-pixel-moving render is even constructible in marker_zoo. The splat docstring warns that grid-snapped edges make consecutive frames byte-identical and the capture dedup collapses them, so --alias must alias spatially while still moving temporally. Untried; it is the load-bearing assumption of the whole synthetic arm.
- The size of the domain gap. All paired (aliased, reference) data comes from marker_zoo; the deployment content is Honkai and BF6 frames the operator cannot supersample. Nothing measures how far a head trained on value-noise-plus-markers is from a real game frame, and there is no way to measure it directly — only the real arm's no-reference probes can contradict the synthetic arm, never confirm it.
- Whether a hand-written CMAA2/SMAA-class pass already passes A1-a and A1-b on his content. Never measured here. If it does, the learned head has no reason to exist and the project ends in three days with a shippable result.
- Whether the AA pass belongs before or after the warp. Before, it is computed 30/s and the antialiased edges are then bilinearly resampled at a displaced coordinate — the FILTER_PLAN records a measured 1.67 px low-phase bias at that hook and calls the pre-warp position 'strictly worse' for an ink line. After, it is computed 240/s at 8x the duty. Nobody has measured which loses less.
- What FLIP's Windows/Vulkan story is. The published protocol is FLIP-based and FLIP ships in C++/NumPy/PyTorch, but this rig's Python is documented as 'numpy 2.5.2 and nothing else'. Whether the gate runs FLIP or falls back to a numpy-only stand-in (per-pixel std + edge width + Sobel) is unresolved, and the fallback is weaker but not disqualifying.
- Whether binding 11's iGPU Sobel field is at the right resolution and semantics to serve as an edge prior for the head. FILTER_PLAN calls this 'UNRESOLVED and the single most promising place the iGPU could pay for itself', and it is read misaligned under --warp-scale N>1.
- Whether the arXiv 2606.05981 the 18.34/12.96 precedent comes from actually exists. It is quoted only from this repo's own DLSS5_RECON.md and I did not verify it independently. The RULE it produced is sound on its own merits (E_warp rewards smearing is derivable), but the specific numbers should not be re-quoted externally until the paper is checked.
- How the AA head would behave at scene cuts, and whether cut detection is needed before A1 or after. The recon records that warp-based stabilizers go blind at cuts (LPIPS jumps ~50 percent while warping error stays flat), and PhyriadFG hits menus and alt-tab constantly. No cut arm is in the proposed corpus and adding one is a fifth day.
