## 1. THE ANSWER IN FIVE LINES

1. **Yes** — he can build a prompt-driven restyling filter into PhyriadFG on a 4090, at **full 1920×1080**, holding **240 Hz output**, but only for the cheap tiers: a prompt→LUT colour/tone grade fused into the existing warp kernel, plus one standalone spatial pass for outlines and flattening.
2. The ceiling is not artistic, it is **one measured number: ~0.40 ms of GPU time per dispatch**. Below it, a pass survives a saturated 4090 with *no* penalty; above it, the same pass is preempted and costs **5.6–8.4 ms** — a cliff, not a slope (MEASURED first-hand, this 4090, driver 610.88, 100% competing load).
3. That cliff buys exactly **one** 64-wide fully-connected neural layer at 1080p (**0.3372 ms MEASURED under 100% contention — no penalty**), or two layers at 720p. Four layers at 1080p costs **8.38 ms MEASURED** and is dead.
4. Diffusion is out at **every** rate on this hardware, not marginally: one distilled denoising step is **10.65 ms at 512×512 on a bare 4090** (measured, recon brief), which is 2.5× the entire 240 Hz frame budget at an eighth of the pixels. Extrapolated to 1080p: **≤7 Hz**.
5. **The single biggest constraint is not the filter — it is that on a heavy game the 4090 has zero headroom already.** BF6 combat, measured in this repo: 4090 at 99%, `warp_ms` 1.2 → 5.2 ms, presented 240 → ~100 fps, FG multiplier collapsed to ~1.07×, *before any filter exists*. On a heavy title the honest answer to "can I also restyle it" is **no**, and the filter must be built to shed itself rather than to pretend otherwise.

## 2. WHAT THE RESEARCH CHANGED

### 2.1 The finding that reverses the original worry

The original fear was that a DLSS-5-class technique needs engine-internal data — G-buffer, depth, geometry — which a capture-based overlay can never have without hooking the game, and that hooking is what anti-cheat kills.

**That fear is void.** NVIDIA's own ADLR page states the inference contract: DLSS 5 is conditioned on *"the current rendered frame, engine motion vectors, carried temporal state, and artistic-direction values."* No G-buffer. No depth. No geometry. The albedo and normals in the marketing are named explicitly as **training-time** supervision: *"During training, consistency supervision from renderer-derived scene attributes further grounds generation to remain faithful to the authored scene."*
Source: https://research.nvidia.com/labs/adlr/DLSS5/ (the linked report PDF at `.../files/DLSS5_Report.pdf` returned HTTP 403 to automated fetch — **open it in a browser; it is item #1 on the reading list and its contents are unverified by us**).

**Colour frame + motion vectors + carried temporal state is precisely and exactly what PhyriadFG already has.** Verified in the code, not inferred: `shaders/fg_core.comp` binds `u_prev_real`/`u_cur_real` (RGBA8 full-res), `u_motion_vectors` (RG16F forward, pixel units), `u_motion_vectors_bwd` (RG16F, cur→prev), `u_dissidence`/`u_dissidence_bwd`, `u_persistence` (per-block static-history counter), `u_candidates` (runner-up MV), and `u_prev_out` (previous output frame, binding 13, refreshed every tick by the `vkCmdCopyImage` at `src/present/present.cpp:1314`). The tensor set this whole class of technique wants is already resident on the 4090, one dispatch away from the final store.

So: **the anti-cheat constraint does not disqualify this class of technique.** PhyriadFG never hooks, never injects, and does not need to. This is the load-bearing good news and it should be stated first.

### 2.2 The finding that closes a door

**DLSS 5 has no text-prompt conditioning at all.** Its "artistic-direction values" are scalars — two separate intensity floats, per the recon; the SIGGRAPH 2026 session reporting describes per-scene / per-material / per-object *constraint* controls, not prompts (session: "Next Era of Graphics: Neural Rendering, World Models, and Simulation", 2026-07-20, Edward Liu et al.; corroborated across TechPowerUp, Guru3D — secondary sources only, NVIDIA has published no document naming Models A/B/C).

Therefore what the operator wants is **a departure, not a re-implementation.** He is not rebuilding DLSS 5 with a text box bolted on; he is building a different thing that borrows DLSS 5's *input contract* and *pipeline position*. That reframing matters, because it means the DLSS 5 literature stops being a blueprint after the input contract, and the text-conditioning half has to come from a different lineage entirely (CLIPstyler → FastCLIPstyler; see §9).

**And the obvious way to do it is excluded by measurement, not by pessimism.** The numbers, with their conditions:

| Claim | Number | Conditions |
|---|---|---|
| One distilled denoising step | **10.65 ms** | 512×512, RTX 4090, TensorRT, **no game running** — MEASURED (recon) |
| Best open streaming video diffusion (StreamDiffusionV2) | **24 fps needs FOUR RTX 4090s** | 512×512 — MEASURED, https://arxiv.org/html/2511.07399v1 |
| Best single-4090 prompt-conditioned stylizer | **54.9 fps** but at **0.5–1.0 s end-to-end latency** | 512×512, batch 8–16 — MEASURED, https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05981v1 |
| Krea Realtime 14B (flagship open-weights) | 11 fps, **40 GB VRAM** | single B200; a 24 GB 4090 cannot load it — vendor, https://www.krea.ai/blog/krea-realtime-14b |
| Anything at 1080p on one consumer GPU | **nobody has published one** | every credible 2026 figure is 512×512, 480p, 368×640 or 320×576 |

The 0.5–1.0 s latency deserves its own sentence: it comes from batching 8–16 frames, and for an overlay whose entire value is frame freshness it is not a tuning problem but a category error. The operator already made this exact trade himself — `--present-waitable` at 99.85% fresh over the async default's ~49.9% (measured, `docs/planning/records/R4_GATE.md:268`).

**Diffusion at gameplay rates is off the table. Do not plan around it, do not plan a "reduced-resolution diffusion mode" as a shipping path.** If he wants to explore it at all it is an offline/screenshot mode, explicitly not the overlay.

### 2.3 Three corrections the research produced that the brief did not have

- **The iGPU is currently doing nothing.** The brief says "device G, currently convert/pack". The code says otherwise: with the 1080 Ti gone, Vulkan enumerates no second discrete device, so `pB` is null and `core_init.cpp:94` derives `single_gpu = true` **with no flag**; `:149` then forces `convert_gpu = CG_PRIMARY` and `:210-213` unconditionally forces `--no-upscale`. The G VDev is created at `:145` and used for nothing; the convert rides `A.q2` on the 4090.
- **`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md` is stale** on hardware topology — it still assigns FLOW to the 1080 Ti (`:20-27`). Under single-GPU the flow pipeline is routed to device A and dispatches on `A.q2`, serialised against the convert by `a_q2_mtx` (`src/flow/flow_init.cpp:31-33`). This is the LEARNING_LOG's own P-018 pattern: a dated document read as live state.
- **Cooperative matrix works today with no build change** — and this reverses one scoping pass's conclusion. One pass grepped the tree (zero hits), read `CMakeLists.txt:63,292` compiling every shader at `--target-env=vulkan1.2`, and concluded a Vulkan 1.3 bump was required. A second pass **measured it**: `glslc 1.4.357.0 --target-env=vulkan1.2` compiles both `GL_KHR_cooperative_matrix` and `GL_NV_cooperative_vector`, emits SPIR-V 1.5 with `OpCapability CooperativeMatrixKHR`, and `spirv-val --target-env vulkan1.2` passes. **The measurement supersedes the inference.** The only change needed is enabling the device extension, and there is an exact in-tree template for that (§7).

## 3. THE LADDER — three tiers, with costs and what each delivers of a cartoon look

Baselines every cost is judged against:

- **Frame budget:** 4.167 ms at 240 Hz (DISPLAY1), 5.556 ms at 180 Hz (DISPLAY2). Shared by capture, flow, warp, present **and the game**.
- **PhyriadFG's own per-tick cost, MEASURED** (Honkai: Star Rail, GPU-*light* scene, 120 fps hardlock, `--force-single-gpu`, 1080p, PresentMon 2.4.1 + internal `--csv` + nvidia-smi, `docs/BENCHMARKS.md §13`): `iter_ms` **2.73 ms at 95% util / 294 W** at `--flow-scale 1` (default); **0.94 ms at 56% util / 138 W** at `--flow-scale 2`. Game alone: 38% util / 185 W.
- **The whole existing warp batch's GPU execution, MEASURED:** **0.08–0.11 ms** (`--warp-timing` GPU timestamps, `R4_GATE.md:177,217-220`, ball_zoo synthetic source, **no game running**). Note the same stats line shows `warp 3.42 ms` — that is submit-to-fence latency, not GPU work. Never quote `warp_ms` as a GPU cost.
- **The contention cliff, MEASURED first-hand on this 4090 (2026-09-07):** under `gpu_load.exe --gpu 0 --load 99` (nvidia-smi-confirmed 100% util, 2925–2940 MHz), dispatches of 0.262 / 0.322 / 0.362 / **0.400 ms took no penalty at all**; at 0.42 ms-equivalent geometry the same shader cost **5.653 ms**. Reproduced with two shaders at three resolutions. The penalty magnitude above the cliff tracks the *competing* workload's submission granularity (8.38 ms at `--heavy 4`, 3.66 ms at `--heavy 1`), so the cliff's **existence** is solid and its **penalty size on a real game is not**.

### Tier 1 — prompt → 3D LUT, fused into `fg_core.comp` as a COMPOSE row

**Cost: +0.02 to +0.05 ms/tick (ESTIMATE)** at 1920×1080 on a 4090 also running a game. Scaled from the measured 0.08–0.11 ms of the *entire* existing warp batch, which already does 20+ texture ops per pixel; the LUT adds one trilinear 3D fetch inside a shader whose store already happens, so it adds no bandwidth. A 33³ RGBA8 LUT is 140 KB — permanently L2-resident on AD102's 72 MB L2.
**Output rate it forces: none. 240 Hz holds, 180 Hz holds, with the cost lost in the noise.**

**Delivers:** posterised/banded shading, a shifted and limited palette, heavy saturation and vibrance, crushed midtones, split-toning, ink-black/paper-white endpoints. If his mental image of *estilo caricaturizado* is mostly a bold flat palette, this alone will surprise him for essentially zero cost.

**CANNOT deliver — say this plainly: Tier 1 cannot draw outlines. Not with a bigger LUT, not with a better prompt, not ever.** An outline is a function of the **local gradient**; a LUT sees one pixel with no neighbours, so a dark pixel beside a bright one is arithmetically indistinguishable from the same dark pixel inside a uniformly dark region. It also cannot flatten texture (that is spatial smoothing), cannot make a lit surface one flat colour (posterising a gradient yields *bands following luminance*, not regions), and cannot do line weight or hatching. Those four gaps are the entire reason Tier 1b exists.

**One correction to a load-bearing recon premise, worth a LEARNING_LOG entry.** "A LUT is a pure per-pixel function that CANNOT flicker" is true about *state* and false as a *stability guarantee*. The LUT adds no temporal state, but output stability depends on |f′|, and a hard N-step quantiser has |f′| → ∞ at every one of its N−1 step edges. Posterisation is therefore the single LUT operation that amplifies input jitter without bound — and the input carries TAA/grain/dither plus warp interpolation error, guaranteed. That is classic banding crawl. The fix costs one parameter (`tone_soft`: smoothstep the steps) plus optionally a **static** blue-noise offset (static, because a fixed spatial pattern is temporally stable by construction). Cheap, but it has to be designed in, not discovered. **UNVERIFIED:** whether the crawl is actually visible at 240 Hz — high refresh may hide it or may land the flicker in the most visible band. One `--outdump` run on a slow luminance ramp settles it.

### Tier 1b — the minimum spatial pass: 5×5 DoG/Sobel edge on luma + soft quantise + LUT

**Cost: 0.10 to 0.20 ms/tick (ESTIMATE)** at 1920×1080 on a 4090 also running a game. One fused compute pass, `wapOutA → wapStyA`, 25 luma taps sharing a 12×12 halo per 8×8 workgroup (L1/L2-hot), one LUT fetch, one store. Comfortably **under the measured 0.40 ms cliff**.
**Output rate: 240 Hz holds.** At `--flow-scale 2` (`iter` 0.94 ms) the tick goes to ~1.1 ms of 4.167 ms.

**Delivers:** the actual outlines. Plus tone flattening if a separable 5×5 bilateral is added — **that variant costs 0.30 to 0.55 ms total (ESTIMATE)**, still under the cliff, still 240 Hz at `--flow-scale 2`.

**Cannot deliver:** semantic/content-adaptive outlining. A gradient operator inks *every* gradient, which includes texture noise, warp ghosts and disocclusion seams. It has no idea what an object is.

**Explicitly NOT recommended: anisotropic Kuwahara** (8 sectors, radius 4, structure-tensor prepass), the "proper" painterly flattener. **0.8 to 1.6 ms/frame (ESTIMATE)** — 20–40% of the whole 240 Hz budget for one cosmetic pass, and over the cliff. The separable bilateral buys roughly 80% of the flattening for ~25% of the cost. Keep Kuwahara **offline**, where it has a real job as Tier 2's teacher.

### Tier 2 — a small purpose-built neural head, in-shader via cooperative matrix

This tier is where the estimates end and the measurements begin, because one scoping pass built and ran it on this exact 4090.

**MEASURED (RTX 4090, driver 610.88, Vulkan SDK 1.4.357.0, device timestamps, best of 28 reps):** a genuine per-pixel head — 8 channels read from VRAM, expanded to a 64-wide feature in shared memory, N fully-connected 64×64 layers on cooperative matrix with ReLU between, 4 channels written back, 16 200 workgroups for 1920×1080:

| Depth | 1080p, **idle** 4090 | 1080p, **contended** 4090 (100% competing load) |
|---|---|---|
| 1 layer | **0.3772 ms** | **0.3372 ms — NO penalty** (faster than idle: the competing load pins clocks at boost) |
| 2 layers | **0.7238 ms** | over the cliff |
| 4 layers | **1.4075 ms** | **8.38 ms** (`--heavy 4`) / **3.66 ms** (`--heavy 1`) |
| 2 layers @ 720p | — | **0.292 ms — no penalty** |

**So Tier 2 at 1080p is exactly one 64-wide layer per dispatch: 0.3372 ms MEASURED under saturation.**
**Output rate it forces:** at `--flow-scale 2` (`iter` 0.94 ms) the tick is 1.28 ms of 4.167 ms — **240 Hz holds**. At two layers 1080p it goes over the cliff and the measured 5.85 ms forces roughly **147 Hz**; at four layers, **107 Hz**. At `--flow-scale 1` the arithmetic says it still fits, and the arithmetic is fiction — that config is already measured at 95% util, the regime where `BENCHMARKS §11` shows `warp_ms` ballooning 1.2 → 5.2 ms and presented fps collapsing 240 → ~100.

**Delivers, over Tier 1b:** the expensive offline filter's quality at the cheap tier's price; content-adaptive outlining that follows perceived object boundaries instead of raw gradient (exactly where the hand-written filter fails); and **temporal stability bought at training time** with an optical-flow warping loss, costing nothing at runtime.

**Cannot deliver:** promptability, without extra work. One trained net is one style. Either ship N nets (a 100K-param FP16 net is ~200 KB — twenty of them is 4 MB) or one net with FiLM/AdaIN modulation from a style embedding (+10–20% params, zero per-frame cost because the modulation coefficients are computed once per prompt change).

**And the honest open question, which no cost measurement touches: whether a one-layer 64-wide head has the capacity for a convincing cartoon look at all.** Everything above bounds the *cost* envelope. Nothing establishes the *quality*. That needs a trained model, not a benchmark.

### Tier 3 — diffusion. Out. See §2.2. ≤7 Hz at 1080p, off by two to three orders of magnitude.

### A disagreement worth naming

One scoping pass estimated the Tier-2 CNN at ~0.74 ms idle / ~1.2 ms contended by scaling a mobile-GPU anchor (138K params, 26.56 ms at 1080p on an Adreno 750) by a ~36× FP16 throughput ratio and then by 1/(1−0.38) for game contention. The *idle* half landed within 3% of the measured 2-layer figure (0.7238 ms) — a genuinely good extrapolation. **The contention half is refuted by measurement:** contention on this GPU is not a linear derating, it is binary. Below ~0.40 ms it costs nothing; above it, 8–20×. Any future budgeting must use the cliff model, not a multiplier.

## 4. THE DESIGN DECISION THAT MATTERS MOST — where the filter hooks

**This is where the scoping passes genuinely disagreed, and the disagreement is substantive.** Both positions, then the call.

### The three candidate hook points

**(A) Before FLOW / at pair upload** — filter `wapCurA` once per pair, at `present.cpp:716-717`, and let the warp propagate the style.

**(B) After GENERATE** — filter `wapOutA` once per tick, at the `--afill` slot in `cmdBridge` (`present.cpp:1210-1228`), after the warp dispatch and before the GENERAL→TRANSFER_SRC blit barrier.

**(C) In PRESENT** — on the shared bridge image. **Ruled out on evidence already paid for.** The in-source postmortem at `present.cpp:552-556` and again at `:1230-1233` records that the historical DEVICE_LOST was a compute stamp on the *shared* bridge texture, and that the in-place-on-`wapOutA` pattern was adopted as the safe alternative. Do not relitigate.

### The case for (A), and it is stronger than it first looks

Two scoping passes independently arrived at (A), and they found a real structural gift.

**The shipping default's output is a function of `u_cur_real` alone.** Verified by reading the generated compose chain and the row bodies, not the docs: `chain_compose.glsl` is `c_out = core.color` → `select` (260) → `stasis` (280) → `single_track` (290), and `single_track` is a **declared OVERRIDE** — its header says "c_in is deliberately discarded". Its body:

```glsl
vec4 r = ctx.B_samp;                                          // B_samp = texture(u_cur_real, uv + mv*(1-t))
const vec4  cur0   = texture(u_cur_real, ctx.uv);
const float d_zero = length(cur0.rgb - texture(u_prev_real, ctx.uv).rgb);
float w_s = smoothstep(1.2, 3.0, (ctx.d_pixel + 0.02) / (d_zero + 0.02));
if (ctx.stasis_block) w_s = 1.0;
r = mix(ctx.B_samp, cur0, w_s);
```

**Both** terms of the mix sample `u_cur_real`. `u_prev_real` contributes exactly one scalar. So styling one image per *pair* styles 100% of the output, exactly, with no cross-frame blend to break style consistency inside a pair — and the warp resamples that styled image every tick for free. Measured cadence in `R4_GATE.md`: 240 ticks/s against 59–61 captured pairs/s, a 4× divider. The temporal-transport problem the recon calls the hardest part is, *within a pair*, already solved by the architecture that exists.

Cost consequence: the same Tier-2 head that costs 0.337 ms/tick at (B) costs 0.337 ms/**pair** at (A) — ~44 GPU-ms/s at a 60 fps source instead of ~81 ms/s. And note the amortisation runs *opposite* to intuition: the slower the game runs, the cheaper the filter gets per presented frame.

The obvious objection to (A) — "posterised frames would wreck the block matcher" — **is false, and the reason is a nice property of the architecture.** `hostR[s]` is imported three times (`core_init.cpp:431-439`); thread F uploads `hR_b` into its own `Bframe` (`flow.cpp:1094`); thread P uploads `hR_a` into `wapPrevA`/`wapCurA` (`present.cpp:716-717`). A GPU pass on the P-side pair **physically cannot reach the flow.** Flow is structurally immune.

### The case for (B), which is the one that wins

Three objections to (A), in ascending force.

**First, it decalibrates the warp's own colour-conditioned machinery.** Flow is immune; the *warp* is not. `mv_guided` (registry rank 10, default ON) and `mv_edge_snap` variant 2 (rank 5) read bindings 0/1 to pick the MV-grid corner whose **colour class** matches the pixel; the `mv_consensus` pass (`mv_median.comp`, dispatched at `present.cpp:766-786`) reads `wapCurA` for the same colour-membership purpose. Posterisation is precisely the operation that collapses colour classes. This is *fixable* — write the styled result to a separate image and keep the guidance rows on the unstyled reals; binding **14 is free** (verified by reading `fgcore_create`: `bd[15]` covers 0..13 plus 15, `bindingCount=15`, no 14 anywhere in shaders or source) — but it costs six edits including a descriptor-layout change, a new 8.3 MB image, and permanent care about which tap reads which image.

**Second, and this is decisive: (A) hands your crisp ink lines to a bilinear resampler with a measured 1.67 px bias.** `B_samp` is `texture(u_cur_real, uv + mv*(1-t))` — a bilinear fetch at a displaced coordinate. A cartoon outline is a **1–2 px feature**. The shipping default's presented positional error is **1.668 px at low phase** (MEASURED, `M1_LOWPHASE_FINDING.md §2`, synthetic zoo, 2 independent 16-triple captures), and the error is **biased, not random** — 88–100% of markers displace *forward* along motion. That is enough to walk an outline clean off the edge it belongs to, on every moving object, every pair. The MV grid is also 8×8 px (`OpticalFlowPipeline.hpp`), so the outline is 4–8× finer than the flow's resolution floor, and the default `mv_median` consensus pass actively *votes away* an isolated small mover's vector. **You would be paying to draw sharp ink and then handing it to a filter whose job is to smooth.**

**Third, (B) has a working precedent in the exact command buffer with the exact barrier pattern, and (A) has none.** `--afill` and `--fps-overlay` both dispatch compute onto `wapOutA` in `cmdBridge` after the warp and before the blit barrier: COMPUTE→COMPUTE `VkMemoryBarrier` (SHADER_WRITE → SHADER_READ|SHADER_WRITE), bind, push, `vkCmdDispatch((WW_warp+7)/8, (WH_warp+7)/8, 1)`. `wap_fill.comp`'s own header states the design rule: *"Runs in-place on the warp output between the warp dispatch and the present blit (the same 4090 graphics-queue submit, no host bounce, sub-frame compute latency)."* And the fps-overlay's twin-descriptor-set pattern (`ov_set_wap` on `wapOutA`, `ov_set` on `Apresent`) is exactly what a stylizer needs if the non-default present paths are ever used.

Cost is (B)'s only real weakness, and the measurements say it does not bite: the LUT tier is ~30× cheaper than the difference, and Tier 1b sits comfortably under the cliff at 240 ticks/s.

### THE DECISION

**Hook AFTER GENERATE, and split the filter in two halves that hook differently.**

**The per-pixel half goes INSIDE `fg_core.comp` as a COMPOSE row — not a new dispatch.** `PFG_LAYER(STYLIZE_LUT, "stylize_lut", COMPOSE, 300, ...)` in `src/control/layer_table.def`, body `shaders/layers/stylize_lut.glsl`, signature `vec4 pfg_compose_stylize_lut(vec4 c_in, in LayerCtx ctx)`, exactly like `select.glsl` and `stasis.glsl`. Why this and not a pass:

- **Zero extra dispatch, zero extra bandwidth** — the store already happens; you add one trilinear fetch to a shader already doing ~20 texture ops.
- **Off means gone.** The registry's contract, quoted from its own header: *"a disabled row is a specialization constant folded at pipeline creation"* — dead code, not a runtime branch. That is how the byte-identical-when-off rule is honoured for free.
- One `PFG_LAYER` row generates the CLI flag, the help text, the JSON UI model, the parity hash and the dependency cascade; `PFG_PARAM` rows generate the LayerParams UBO slots at binding 15. One declaration site — the project's whole discipline.
- **The reason that matters most:** inside the kernel the row has `ctx.warp_ok`, `ctx.d_pixel` and `ctx.stasis_block`. That is an **artifact-aware gate the standalone pass cannot have.** Fade tone quantisation toward the unstyled value where `warp_ok` is false and the filter stops shouting on exactly the pixels the warp is least sure about. This is the direct mitigation of (B)'s honest cost.
- Binding 14 is free, and the pipeline's existing sampler is already `VK_FILTER_LINEAR` with `addressModeU = addressModeV = addressModeW = CLAMP_TO_EDGE` (verified in `fgcore_create`) — `addressModeW` is the only thing a 2D-only sampler would be missing, so **a 3D LUT drops in with no new sampler and no layout redesign.**
- One constraint to declare rather than discover: `--legacy-warp` cannot carry this. `wap_warp.comp` is at 232 B of a 256 B push limit and has no LayerParams UBO. Declare `requires fg_core` in the registry so `resolve_config`'s self-audit says so out loud instead of the flag being a silent no-op.

**The spatial half goes in the standalone slot**, `shaders/stylize_spatial.comp`, dispatched exactly where `--afill` is. One difference from `--afill` that matters: `--afill` is per-pixel and therefore race-free in place; **a spatial pass reads neighbours and cannot be in-place.** Dispatch `wapOutA → wapStyA` (new image, +8.3 MB VRAM) and re-point the blit source at `present.cpp:1299` from `wapOutA` to `wapStyA`. **Do not copy back** — that would add a full 8.3 MB image copy for nothing.

**What this choice costs, stated plainly:** the stylizer runs on an image containing warp artifacts, so a posterise-and-outline filter will ink around ghosts and disocclusion seams, amplifying exactly the errors R0–R7 spent months suppressing. That is real, and the `warp_ok`-gated fade in the fused row is the mitigation. **Revisit (A) only if a measured Tier-2 cost forces the amortisation** — and price the revisit honestly: it buys 2–4× cost reduction and within-pair exactness, and it pays with resampled outlines and a decalibration hazard around three MV rows.

**Keep the flow on unstyled reals, permanently.** Under (B) this is automatic — the flow never sees the stylizer's output. Do not build a feedback path. **UNESTABLISHED:** what a styled image does to the matcher's SAD statistics, the gme affine fit's residual scale, `kChangeGateSadZ = 0.5` and the stasis threshold 0.50 — all calibrated on real-content contrast, all would need re-deriving. Sidestep it.

## 5. TEMPORAL STABILITY — why this codebase starts ahead, and where it breaks

### Where it starts ahead, by file

- **A dense forward AND backward flow field, device-resident, per pair.** `u_motion_vectors` (binding 2) and `u_motion_vectors_bwd` (binding 5), RG16F, full-res **pixel** units, on a W/8 × H/8 grid = **240×135 at 1080p** (`framework/render/vulkan/include/phyriad/render/vulkan/OpticalFlowPipeline.hpp:228-247`; grid enforced by a runtime FATAL assert at `src/flow/flow_init.cpp:48-53`). Bidirectional is default-on (`layer_table.def` rank 30 BIDIR). Total: ~0.55 MB for all seven MV-grid fields.
- **The literature pays dearly for exactly this.** The 2026 streaming-stylisation paper warps the previous decoded frame with optical flow as its temporal conditioning, and its flow computation was **96% of an 882 ms latency spike** (CPU Farnebäck), forcing 8 worker threads and async prefetch to get p95 from 1439 ms down to 756 ms (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05981v1). PhyriadFG computes the same signal on the GPU, per pair, as part of its shipping path. **This is the single strongest asset he has.**
- **Raw flow accuracy is good:** endpoint error **0.464 px mean / 0.180 px median, 0.164 px on the linear class** — MEASURED, synthetic `zoo_static`, 15 markers 6–24 px at 1.75–8 px/frame, MV sampled at each marker vs analytic displacement, 2 independent 16-triple captures (`M1_LOWPHASE_FINDING.md §1`).
- **A previous-output history image, already maintained per tick at zero new cost:** binding 13 `u_prev_out`, refreshed by `vkCmdCopyImage(cmdBridge, wapOutA → wapPrevOutA)` at `present.cpp:1314` under `--ts-smooth`. If posterise crawl ever needs a temporal EMA, the resource exists.
- **And the free win for a feed-forward net:** temporal stability in this family is bought at **training** time with an optical-flow warping loss (https://arxiv.org/pdf/1807.01197, from https://arxiv.org/abs/1604.08610) and costs **nothing at runtime**. He can generate that loss's warps offline from his own field, for free. **CAVEAT:** the Ruder abstract does not itself state the loss is flow-based with occlusion masks; confirm the mechanism in the PDF before relying on it.

### Where estimated flow breaks — the honest list

1. **Thin geometry — the failure that specifically kills a cartoon look.** The MV grid tile is 8×8 px; a cartoon outline is 1–2 px, i.e. **4–8× finer than the flow's resolution floor**. Worse, MEASURED (`S2_T5_GATE.md §2.1`): 24 px markers land most precisely (0.24–0.31 px) and are degraded most often (20 of 32 linear); 6 px markers land least precisely (0.84–0.91 px) and are degraded least — a large marker spans several tiles that disagree and tear it, a small one sits inside one tile and is carried whole to a *coarser* position.
2. **The default consensus pass makes it worse, measurably.** The shipping default presents 1.668 px of forward drag at low phase; with the 3×3 vector-median consensus off (`--no-mv-guided`) the error is **0.271 px, flat across phase** (MEASURED, same protocol, 2 runs: 0.267 / 0.301). Mechanism, isolated: on a 6–24 px marker crossing a static background the marker's tile *is* the stray vector, its eight neighbours are static, the median is ~0, and the marker's motion is voted away. **The consensus default flip is already on the operator's own open list; the filter work makes his pending decision load-bearing.** Note it is not a free switch — `--no-mv-guided` also disables the colour-guided corner pick.
3. **Disocclusion — unhandled on the default path.** This one corrects the brief. The fwd↔bwd round-trip classification, fill-div, matte, background-snap, band-crossfade and disocclusion-commit live **only** in `shaders/wap_warp.comp` (gated at `:697-701` on `occl_thresh > 0`), which is not the default kernel. The generated `layer_manifest.txt` lists all 28 rows and **contains no occlusion row at all.** Worse: `warp_ok` (Gate 1 confidence AND Gate 2 tile agreement) is computed and then **thrown away** — it feeds only `select` at rank 260, whose output the rank-290 override discards. The only residual protection is `w_s` in `single_track`, a *photometric* evidence mix, not a geometric one. **A filter gets far less protection for free than the brief implies.**
4. **Fast camera motion — a hard measured wall.** Matcher coarse reach is ±48 px per pair at `flow_div=1` (±6×8, documented design fact). Beyond ~12–16 px/frame the error is **5.2 px with 29 of 96 markers simply absent**, both runs agreeing perfectly (r = 1.00).
5. **HUD/UI — good news measured, bad news structural.** The screen-static evidence mix keeps a screen-fixed HUD at **0.046 px over a 120 px/s pan, 35/36 found** — the best-behaved class in the baseline. But it is **ONE RUN, stamped r = n/a by the report itself**; do not treat it as a baseline. And there is no semantic separation available: a style filter restyles the HUD along with the scene. `wap_warp.comp:1315` also records a known limit — content moving exactly one texture period per pair aliases `d_zero` to ~0.
6. **Scene cuts — no detector exists, and PhyriadFG hits them constantly** (menus, loading screens, cutscenes, alt-tab). The recon flags cuts as an unsolved hazard for every warp- and cache-based stabiliser, with LPIPS-against-oracle jumping ~50% at hard cuts *while warping error stays flat and hides it.*

### The cheapest scene-cut detector, riding entirely on data already computed

**Two scalars on the F thread. Zero GPU work, zero new buffers, zero new passes.**

- (a) mean `sad_zero` over the 240×135 grid **saturates** (content changed everywhere), AND
- (b) the match explains nothing — mean `sad_best` / mean `sad_zero` → 1, i.e. the improvement gate `sad_zero*(1-improvement_frac) > sad_best` fails across the grid, AND
- (c) gme dissidence % is already high (an affine model fits nothing across a cut).

(a) and (b) are one extra accumulation inside a loop that **already walks the decoded grid**: `gme_fit_affine` decodes the whole fp16 MV grid and the whole `sad_zero` grid into thread-local float scratch `g_gme_szf` every pair via the F16C batch (`src/flow/flow.cpp:35-46`, `:47-131`). (c) is already computed (`warp_blend.cpp:24-29`, `gme_dispct_from_mask`) and already published to the present thread (`flow_consume.cpp:335-341`). Cost: **~32 400 float accumulations per pair (ESTIMATE of the arithmetic; the data is already resident) — sub-microsecond class on a modern core, unmeasured.**

**Reuse the `--motion-fallback` latch verbatim** (`present.cpp:2302-2314`): EMA α = 0.2, Schmitt latch, `kMfHyst = 12.0` dispersion-percent deadband, `kMfDwell = 4` ticks. It is already tuned against flapping. On a cut: drop styled history, re-filter, and (for Tier 2) reset any carried state.

**Make it default ON.** And fix the doc/code contradiction while there: `cli.hpp:779-785` documents `--motion-fallback` as "DEFAULT ON" and `cli.cpp:332` prints "DEFAULT es ON", but the initialiser is `false`, `resolve_config` never sets it true, and `cli.cpp:439` says "DEFAULT OFF". Runtime behaviour is OFF. **UNRESOLVED:** whether that is a doc bug or a deliberate later flip with stale comments — the history lives in the child repo and was not consulted.

### And the metric warning, which fits this project's own discipline

**Warping error as a flicker metric rewards smearing.** The 2026 paper documents a configuration that improved ε_w from 18.34 to 12.96 **while collapsing Sobel edge magnitude by 60%** — a metric win that was a quality loss. Any gate he builds must pair a temporal metric with a **spatial-detail probe**, exactly the way M4's oracle gate paired k with byte-exactness.

## 6. DEVICE AND BUDGET

### The 4090 alone. The iGPU almost never. Here is the arithmetic rather than a hunch.

**Moving a 1080p frame to the iGPU and back costs ~1.52 ms** — 2 × the **MEASURED 0.76 ms** one-way (2026-06-06, `bench/stage2_image_xfer`, real DEVICE_LOCAL VkImage transfer 4090 → iGPU via `VK_EXT_external_memory_host` shared host buffer, no memcpy, 1080p RGBA8 = 8 MB; 4K was 3.2 ms). That is **36.5% of the entire 240 Hz frame budget spent moving bytes and performing zero filtering** (27% at 180 Hz, 18% at 120 Hz).

**Break-even therefore requires a filter costing more than 1.52 ms of 4090 time.** No tier that fits the budget does: Tier 1 is ~30× cheaper than the transport; Tier 1b is ~5× cheaper; Tier 2 at one layer (0.337 ms measured) is still 4.5× cheaper; Tier 3 does not run anywhere. **The iGPU never breaks even on per-frame work — before its compute is even characterised.**

The codebase already encodes this answer: `core_init.cpp:160-210` hard-codes `fg_ai = 2.5` FLOP/byte against a *~596* FLOP/byte crossover on this machine's link, with the comment that a capability-driven offload decision "returns offload=false ALWAYS". A per-pixel LUT's arithmetic intensity is **lower** than 2.5 — it fails the same test by a wider margin.

**The two secondary roles that do survive:**
1. **The one-shot prompt→LUT bake**, entirely off the critical path, never crossing PCIe per frame.
2. **`shaders/igpu_field.comp` already computes a Sobel contour field of the real frame**, and it is already bridged to the warp at binding 11 when `cfg.d.field_to_warp` is set. That is *half of an edge-detect pass, already computed on the secondary device*. **UNRESOLVED and the single most promising place the iGPU could pay for itself:** whether it is at the right resolution and semantics to serve as a free outline source. Known caveat recorded in-source (`present.cpp:1218-1226`): under `--warp-scale N>1` the field is read misaligned.

**Note also, on the legal side:** anything touching an NVIDIA RTX SDK is foreclosed on the iGPU regardless — Supplement §1 of the RTX SDKs licence: *"the DLSS SDK, NGX SDK and RTX Dynamic Vibrance SDK are licensed for you to develop applications only for their use in systems with NVIDIA GPUs."* Irrelevant to his own original code, which is what we are building.

### The honest trade: a filter costs the game frames, and it compounds

PhyriadFG exists to make motion smoother, and its measured weak points are exactly the ones a filter attacks. On the **lightest** game ever measured on this rig it already takes the 4090 from 38% to 95% util and 185 W to 294 W, at 43.7 ms of added latency. On BF6 combat the 4090 is at 99% and the FG multiplier has already collapsed to ~1.07×.

A filter does not merely trade picture quality for motion. **It takes GPU time from the game → the base frame rate falls → input latency rises AND the FG must synthesise a higher multiplier over fewer real frames → more artifacts → which the filter then inks.** That is a compounding trade, not a linear one. Say it out loud in the docs.

### Recommended defaults

1. **`--stylize` OFF by default, byte-identical when off.** The house rule every layer already follows, and the COMPOSE-row mechanism (specialization constant folded at pipeline creation) gives it for free.
2. **`--stylize` implies `--flow-scale 2`,** expressed as a cascade in `resolve_config()` with the startup self-audit printing why. This is not advice, it is a decision his own measurements make: `iter` 2.73 ms @ 95% util vs 0.94 ms @ 56% util for the same scene, warp 1.41 → 0.47 ms, AddedLatency 43.7 → 13.2 ms. The quality cost is documented (gme dissidence 0.02 → 0.15) and is his call, but the stylize tier does not fit on the 95%-util config in practice regardless of what the arithmetic says.
3. **Wire it into the EXISTING governor as the FIRST rung shed** — `governor_floor_for_util()` / `g_gpu_a_util` (`src/core/globals.hpp:22-34`), ahead of `vblend`, `band-xfade` and `multicand` in the `warp_light` block at `present.cpp:1140-1150`. A cosmetic pass must never survive a saturation event that is collapsing the multiplier.
4. **Arm `--upload-xfer` and re-measure before anything else.** `wap_upload` is already the pipeline's single largest cost — **~10 ms/frame on the 4090 graphics queue at combat load, host-blocked** (MEASURED with `--wsub`, `BENCHMARKS.md §4`) — and the non-xfer path does `vkQueueSubmit(A.q,...)` then `vk_wait_live(A.dev, fBridge)`, a host block (`present.cpp:806-809`). The fix (moving copies to `A.qT`) is implemented and **default OFF** (`cli.hpp:909`).
5. **Refuse to arm Tier 2 above 120 Hz output unless the measured dispatch is under 0.35 ms.** The cliff is a hard gate, not a guideline.
6. **`--warp-scale 2` is the ready-made escape** if a spatial or neural pass is too expensive at full res: the warp runs at WW/2 × WH/2 and the **existing** linear blit upsamples it, no new pass. Default-off because it is documented as blurry; it is a cost lever, not a latency one.

## 7. RUNTIME — how it ships without breaking the single-file model

### The constraint is stricter than "a DLL next to the exe"

`ui.exe` embeds **exactly one** payload — `build.rs` copies `phyriad_fg.exe` into `OUT_DIR` and `lib.rs:19` does `include_bytes!` — and extracts it to `%LOCALAPPDATA%\PhyriadFG\bin\phyriad_fg-<FNV1a>.exe`, running it **from there**, not from beside the launcher. Any added DLL is a second embedded payload plus DLL-search handling. Current sizes: `phyriad_fg.exe` **1 354 752 B**, `ui.exe` **9 048 576 B**. The exe today links only system libraries plus the driver's `vulkan-1.dll` (`CMakeLists.txt:316`), and `dist/README.txt` declares the complete end-user requirement: MSVC 2015+ redistributable and a driver providing `vulkan-1.dll`.

### The decision: hand-written Vulkan compute, `VK_KHR_cooperative_matrix`, `VK_NV_cooperative_vector` as an optional NVIDIA fast path

**Added shipping bytes: ZERO DLL. ~1–4 KB of SPIR-V per shader plus the weights** (32 KB for the measured 4-layer 64×64 FP16 head; ~1 MB for a 1M-parameter FP8 net). The shader goes through the existing `pfg_spv()` rule → `spv_to_header.cmake`, which emits `inline constexpr std::array<uint32_t, N>` and is `#include`d by `main.cpp` — the mechanism 20+ shaders already use. Weights embed through the same byte-blob rule. **The launcher's single payload is untouched, and no new end-user prerequisite appears.**

**MEASURED capability on this exact 4090 (driver 610.88, device API 1.4.341):**
- `VK_KHR_cooperative_matrix` rev 2, `VK_NV_cooperative_matrix`, `VK_NV_cooperative_matrix2`, `VK_NV_cooperative_vector` rev 4, all present.
- **15 cooperative-matrix shapes**, all subgroup scope, including FP16→FP16/FP32, BF16→FP32, int8→u32/s32, and **FP8 E4M3 / E5M2 → FP16/FP32**.
- Cooperative **vector**: `cooperativeVector=1`, `cooperativeVectorTraining=1`, all 14 shader stages (0x3fff), `maxCooperativeVectorComponents=1024`, 16 input/matrix/result combinations.
- **Full tensor-core throughput from a plain compute shader: 340.9 TFLOP/s** FP16→FP16 (NVIDIA's published dense figure for the 4090 is 330.3), 170.6 TFLOP/s FP16→FP32, against a **71.6 TFLOP/s** scalar FP32 FMA control — **4.8× the class of arithmetic the project's shaders use today.** Not an emulation path.
- **No build-system change needed** (contra one scoping pass's inference): the project's existing `--target-env=vulkan1.2` glslc line compiles both extensions and `spirv-val --target-env vulkan1.2` passes.
- The **AMD iGPU also exposes `VK_KHR_cooperative_matrix`** (rev 2, 11 shapes, subgroup 64, 32 KB shared mem) — so the path is not NVIDIA-locked, though it has no cooperative-vector.

**The device-enable is a five-line change with an exact in-tree template.** `src/core/device.cpp` already does precisely this shape for `VK_NV_optical_flow`: scan `vkEnumerateDeviceExtensionProperties` into `d.has_optical_flow`, gate `use_ofa = want_ofa && has_optical_flow`, push the name into `exts`, chain `VkPhysicalDeviceOpticalFlowFeaturesNV` onto `dci.pNext` — and `flow_init.cpp:79-95` prints an honest fallback when the device lacks it ("falling back to the classical OFP (byte-identical)"). **Follow that template and the pass is off by default, byte-identical when disabled, and degrades honestly on a GPU without cooperative matrix.**

**One runtime rule from measurement: the pass must ride the async present path.** A submit-and-wait on a contended 4090 has a **flat 1.85–3.95 ms wall-clock queue-wait floor regardless of dispatch size** (MEASURED for dispatches of 0.057 / 0.112 / 0.179 / 0.355 ms GPU time under 100% load; idle equivalent 0.106–0.434 ms). That is almost certainly the same effect `BENCHMARKS.md §5` already records as *"4.57 ms (~73% fence-wait)"* under combat saturation. `PresentStage::submit()` already sheds it: `if(!tk.ap){ submit; vk_wait_live(...); } else { submit; async_inflight = tk.back; }` with the comment *"No vkWaitForFences here — that wait IS the latency we shed."* The cheapest way to inherit this is to record the filter dispatch in the **same `cmdBridge`** as the warp, which the `--afill` slot does by construction. **UNVERIFIED:** whether the phase/pair state machine tolerates a second dispatch's added latency in the async slot — trace it before landing.

## 8. STAGE 1 — the cheapest experiment that proves or kills it

**Milestone name: `M-S1 — the stylize budget gate`. Buildable in days. It produces numbers, not screenshots.**

### Why this and not "ship the LUT first"

Shipping the LUT first is the *comfortable* road: it is nearly free, it will pass trivially, and it will look like something. But it **proves nothing about the interesting version** — it cannot draw an outline, so it cannot test the one feature that decides whether *estilo caricaturizado* is reachable at all. The three things that can actually kill this project are (a) no headroom on a real game, (b) outlines cannot survive this pipeline, (c) flicker. M-S1 tests all three at once.

### What to build

The minimum spatial pass, behind one flag, at the `--afill` slot: **5×5 DoG/Sobel edge on luma → soft quantise (`tone_soft`, not a hard step) → 33³ LUT apply**, one fused compute dispatch `wapOutA → wapStyA`, re-pointing the blit source. It reuses `--afill`'s exact barrier / bind / push / dispatch sequence and `wap_fill.comp` as the structural template. Bake the LUT on the CPU from a hard-coded StyleSpec — **no prompt machinery, no model, no text encoder in Stage 1.** The prompt is a later, purely host-side concern.

### What to measure — three numbers, all with instrumentation that already exists

Run on a **real game at 240 Hz**, with `--warp-timing --wsub --csv`, at both `--flow-scale 1` and `2`, medians, following `BENCHMARKS.md §13`'s stated protocol.

1. **N1 — the pass's own GPU time under real contention.** `--warp-timing` brackets the whole `cmdBridge` batch with GPU timestamps (`timing_begin` after `vkBeginCommandBuffer`, `timing_end` after the blit), surfacing as ` gpu X.XXms` and CSV column `phyriadfg_warp_gpu_ms`. Baseline to subtract: **0.08–0.11 ms** (the measured empty-batch figure). **No new instrumentation required.**
2. **N2 — the cost to the game.** Presented fps, `slip_ms`, `MsAddedLatency`, `GPUUtilization_4090`, `GPUPower_4090` from the existing `--csv` telemetry plus PresentMon 2.4.1, with the filter on vs off.
3. **N3 — the outline survival number, paired against smearing.** Over a scripted pan: temporal warping error ε_w of the stylized output **AND** mean Sobel edge magnitude of the same frames. Report both. **A ε_w improvement accompanied by an edge-magnitude collapse is a failure, not a success** — this is the recon's documented trap (18.34 → 12.96 while edge magnitude fell 60%) and it is the same discipline M4's oracle gate applied when it paired k with byte-exactness.

**Add, in the same session and for free, the re-baseline that everything else depends on:** run the *current default binary* with no filter on the same real game and record the same counters. **Every cost figure in `docs/BENCHMARKS.md §12-13` is from 2026-06-22 and predates both the R7(a) `fg_core.comp` default (2026-09-06) and the R4 present-stage extraction (2026-09-05).** The shipping binary's real cost on a real game has never been measured. That baseline costs one run and is what the filter must be judged against.

### What result means STOP

- **N1 > ~0.35 ms on a real game** → the pass is over the measured preemption cliff. **Stop the spatial tier.** Fall back to the fused COMPOSE-row LUT only, and tell the operator plainly that the cartoon look is **colour-only** on this hardware at 240 Hz. Tier 2 is dead in the same breath, because its cheapest viable configuration is 0.337 ms.
- **N2: presented fps falls more than ~10% at `--flow-scale 2` on a light game** → this is a 120 Hz feature, not a 240 Hz one. Re-scope to DISPLAY2/120 Hz and say so, rather than shipping something that quietly costs the multiplier the project exists to provide.
- **N3: mean Sobel edge magnitude over the pan falls materially versus the static baseline** → the outline is being destroyed by the pipeline itself. **Stop.** And note the pre-warp hook (option A in §4) does **not** rescue this — it is strictly worse there, because the ink would additionally be bilinearly resampled at a displaced coordinate with a measured 1.67 px low-phase bias.
- **The re-baseline shows less than ~0.3 ms/tick of headroom at `--flow-scale 2` with the game running** → stop before writing any filter at all; the premise is gone and no tier fits.

**If all three pass:** land the fused STYLIZE_LUT COMPOSE row (days), publish the numbers into `docs/BENCHMARKS.md` in §13's format, add the scene-cut detector default-ON, and only then decide whether Tier 2 is worth training a model for.

**One preliminary worth running the same afternoon, because it costs two minutes:** repeat the dispatch-size sweep that located the 0.40 ms cliff, but against a **real game** instead of `gpu_load.exe`. The cliff's *existence* is solid; its penalty magnitude tracks the competing workload's submission granularity (proved by the 8.38 vs 3.66 ms difference between `--heavy 4` and `--heavy 1`), and a game's granularity is not a synthetic spinner's. The benchmark already exists in the scratchpad probe directory.

## APPENDIX — what could not be established, listed rather than smoothed

1. **Whether any cost figure holds on a GPU-heavy game.** Every repo cost number comes from Honkai: Star Rail, explicitly a GPU-light scene (game alone 38% util). `BENCHMARKS §5` separately measured that under saturation **73% of the FG's per-frame slice was fence-wait, not shader time.** The headroom figures are upper bounds a heavy title will not honour. **Cheaply testable and it is Stage 1.**
2. **The penalty magnitude above the 0.40 ms cliff on a real game.** The cliff is measured and reproducible; the 5.6–8.4 ms penalty tracks `gpu_load.exe`'s submission granularity, not a game's.
3. **Whether a 1-layer 64-wide head has the capacity for a convincing cartoon look.** Everything here bounds cost; nothing establishes quality. Needs a trained model.
4. **How much of the 86% efficiency gap in the measured MLP is recoverable** (48.3 of 340.9 TFLOP/s achieved with shared-memory staging, barriers and separate ReLU passes). A register-resident, fused-activation version would be faster by an unguessed factor.
5. **Whether cooperative *vector* beats cooperative *matrix* for this workload on Ada** — cooperative vector removes the full-subgroup and uniform-control-flow requirements, which matters for a per-pixel head with masked regions. Only cooperative matrix was benchmarked. Also unmeasured: the one-time `vkConvertCooperativeVectorMatrixNV` layout-conversion cost.
6. **The actual ticks-per-pair distribution.** S = output_hz / source_fps was derived analytically; the measured 256–284 fps from a 120 fps hardlock implies S ≈ 2.1–2.4, and `present.cpp:2475` uses a variable `span` under an NCO+PLL whose multiplier is documented as "variable, not fixed". Any amortisation over S needs the real distribution **including its tail**, not the mean.
7. **Whether the 0.27 px flat-in-phase result survives on real game content.** Every positional number comes from a synthetic marker zoo with value-noise backgrounds. Known real-content defects (the pan-bench glass-ghost at 134.5 px cited in `wap_warp.comp:1298`, dense foliage, periodic textures the `ambig` row exists to fight) are not in that corpus.
8. **Whether posterise band-crawl is visible at 240 Hz.** Untested; decides whether `tone_soft` is a nice-to-have or load-bearing.
9. **Whether the operator ever runs the non-default present paths** (`--rfp`, `--real-fast-path`, the grid/sync arm, `--legacy-warp`). If not, the twin descriptor set on `Apresent` and the whole "do interpolated frames match real ones" question are dead scope. **Ask him before building the twin.**
10. **Whether the async present slot tolerates a second dispatch** without disturbing the phase/pair state machine.
11. **Whether the iGPU's existing `igpu_field.comp` Sobel contour field is usable as a free outline source** — resolution and semantics unresolved; misaligned under `--warp-scale N>1`.
12. **No CTest, no CI** (`ARCHITECTURE.md`, corrected 2026-09-04: `grep -c "enable_testing\|add_test"` returns 0). Any byte-identical-when-off claim must be gated by hand, the way `--fg-core-ab` was.

- The R4 gate's 0.08-0.11 ms warp-batch GPU time was measured against ball_zoo, a synthetic source, with no game on the GPU. There is no measurement of that same counter with a real game contending. - How a filter interacts with the quality machinery is unexamined: the bidirectional occlusion classifier, the global-motion matte, the dissidence masks and the mass-feedback loop all key on pixel content. - Scene cuts. The recon brief flags them as an unsolved hazard for every warp- and cache-based stabilizer, and PhyriadFG hits them constantly (menus, loading screens, alt-tab). I found no cut-detection in the flow or present paths. This does not affect a stateless LUT (pure per-pixel, cannot flicker), but it would affect any filter carrying temporal state.
- There is no CTest and no CI (docs/ARCHITECTURE.md, corrected 2026-09-04: 'grep -c enable_testing|add_test returns 0'), so any claim that a filter change is byte-identical when off would need to be gated by hand, the way --fg-core-ab was.

**Recommendation:** THE ARITHMETIC, PLAINLY. At 240 Hz the whole machine has 4.16 ms per frame, and on the lightest game ever measured on this rig the FG already takes 0.94-2.73 ms of it while the game takes another 38% of the device. Optimistically that leaves about 60 GPU-milliseconds per second — roughly a quarter of a millisecond per tick. On BF6 it leaves zero, and the FG is already collapsing to a 1.07x multiplier there. So the filter budget is not "small"; at 240 Hz on a heavy game it is nil, and the design must survive that rather than assume it away.

WHERE EACH TIER STOPS FITTING (per-tick placement, on a contended 4090 at 1080p): a 3D LUT (~0.05-0.08 ms/frame, my estimate against a measured 0.08-0.11 ms for the entire existing warp batch) fits at 240, 180, 120 and 60 Hz — it never becomes the constraint. A small purpose-built CNN (~0.74 ms idle, ~1.2 ms contended, my extrapolation) does NOT fit per-tick at 240 Hz: it forces output down to about 80 Hz, below both panels, or takes ~20 percentage points of the 4090 straight out of the game. Anything diffusion-shaped is out at every rate by two to three orders of magnitude — one distilled step measures 10.65 ms at 512x512 on a bare 4090, which is 2.5x the entire 240 Hz budget at an eighth of the pixels.

THE DESIGN MOVE THAT CHANGES THE ANSWER. Do not put the filter after the warp. Put it at present.cpp:717-718, immediately after up_imgA fills wapPrevA and wapCurA — i.e. filter the two REAL frames once per pair advance, and let the warp propagate the style. Three things fall out at once. (1) The cost divides by the FG multiplier: the R4 gate measured 240 ticks/s against 59-61 captured pairs/s, so the same Tier-2 net that forces 80 Hz per-tick costs ~44 GPU-ms/s per-real-frame and 240 Hz survives. (2) Temporal stability comes free and by construction — the intermediate frames are resampled from an already-styled image, not re-inferred, so there is no per-tick style flicker to fight. (3) The optical flow is untouched: thread F rea

- **CORRECTION to the recon's load-bearing premise. 'A LUT is a pure per-pixel function that CANNOT flicker' is true about the LUT adding no state, but false as a stability guarantee for a cartoon look. Posterization is the specific case where a LUT AMPLIFIES input jitter without bound: at a band boundary the quantizer's derivative is infinite, so a pixel whose luminance dithers by one code value flips between two output tones every frame. That is the classic 'banding crawl'.**

The captured frames PhyriadFG stylizes are game frames carrying TAA/film-grain/dither noise plus, in the default path, warp interpolation error, so the input jitter that gets amplified is guaranteed present. PRESENT is out on evidence you already paid for. The in-source postmortem at present.cpp:552 and 1230 says the historical DEVICE_LOST was a compute stamp on the SHARED bridge image, and that the safe pattern is compute-in-place on wapOutA. Do not relitigate that.

The obvious worry — "posterized frames would wreck the block matcher" — is FALSE here, and the reason is a nice property of your architecture: hostR[s] is imported three times (core_init.cpp:431-439), thread F uploads hR_b into its own Bframe (flow.cpp:1094), thread P uploads hR_a into wapPrevA/wapCurA (present.cpp:716-717). A GPU pass on the P-side pair never reaches flow. Flow is structurally immune. What is NOT immune is the warp's OWN colour-conditioned machinery: mv_guided (rank 10) and mv_edge_snap variant 2 (rank 5) read bindings 0/1 to pick the MV grid corner whose COLOUR CLASS matches the pixel, and the mv_consensus pass (rank 90, mv_median.comp at present.cpp:766) reads wapCurA for colour membership. Posterization is precisely the operation that collapses colour classes. You would be feeding your MV arbitration a signal you had just destroyed.

There is a second, independent objection that kills it even if you fixed the first: the warp bilinearly samples its sources and cross-fades them. Stylize before the warp and every crisp outline you draw gets resampled and blended — you would pay to draw sharp ink and then hand it to a filter whose job is to smooth. Outlines must be drawn on final geometry.
