# PhyriadFG — SCOPE DOCUMENT: what a prompt-driven stylizer can actually deliver at 30 fps

**Date:** 2026-09-07 · **Rig:** RTX 4090 (single-GPU to PhyriadFG; the 1080 Ti is in driver error 31), driver 610.88, Vulkan SDK 1.4.357.0, Windows 11 · **Project shape:** one person, C++23/Vulkan, single self-contained .exe, cannot train a model · **Premise, taken as constraint not opinion:** 30 fps or below is a win; free/open pretrained models are viable; training is impossible.

## 1. THE ANSWER IN SIX LINES

1. **You are right to reframe it — but the reframe buys a bigger DEADLINE, not more GPU-seconds, and that single distinction decides everything else in this document.**
2. **What becomes possible:** one distilled denoising step at 512×512 goes from 256% of the per-tick budget to 32% of it (10.65 ms MEASURED, RTX 4090 + TensorRT, no game running); and the cooperative-matrix neural head goes from exactly one 64-wide layer to four or more (8.38 ms MEASURED contended = 201% of a 240 Hz tick, 25% of a 30 fps tick).
3. **What stays impossible:** 1080p diffusion (~84 ms/step ESTIMATE by area scaling = 253% of the whole device at 30 styled frames/s), and BF6 or any heavy shooter (PhyriadFG already MEASURES a 0.84× multiplier there — a net loss — with no filter present at all).
4. **Best achievable result:** a flat-cartoon / cel look, styled at 512×288–512×512, upscaled to 1080p *for free* inside the warp's own bilinear fetch, one styled image per captured pair at ~30/s, presented at 60–120 Hz through the existing warp, on a GPU-light single-player game with its own frame rate capped by the user.
5. **What it costs:** ~18–33% of the 4090 taken from the game (ESTIMATE, scaled from the MEASURED 10.65 ms anchor), ~110–145 ms total added end-to-end latency (ESTIMATE, built on MEASURED 13.2–82.8 ms of PhyriadFG's own added latency), the death of the single self-contained .exe for the styled mode, and a look that is stable on flat colour and boils on invented detail.
6. **And the number that decides whether even that is real is unmeasured:** what a ~10 ms dispatch actually costs on this 4090 while a game is running. Every diffusion figure in the literature is on an idle GPU. That measurement is two hours of work with an instrument you already built. **Do it before anything else.**

## 2. WHAT THE REFRAME CHANGED

### 2.1 The one sentence that organises the rest

A 30 fps target multiplies the **wall-clock deadline** by exactly 8.000× (4.167 → 33.333 ms). It does **not** multiply the GPU-seconds available. Therefore:

- **Everything that was dead because it MISSED A DEADLINE is now alive.**
- **Everything that was dead because it CONSUMES GPU-SECONDS is exactly as dead as it was.**

This is not a rhetorical distinction — it is measured. SATURATION_PLAN §9, live BF6, `--gpu-priority realtime`, paired ~30 s runs: capping PhyriadFG's own output from 240 to 120 returned **nothing** to the game (game arrival 83 → 79 fps, i.e. slightly *worse*; gpu-A 91% → 88%) and made the latency floor worse (min 14.5 → 18.5 ms). MEASURED. The doc's own verdict on its own hypothesis: *refuted*. The saturation tax is capture-side — the WGC `CopyResource` of every **game** frame — so it scales with the game's frame rate, not with ours.

### 2.3 What did NOT flip — and these are the important ones

1. **The cliff is a per-dispatch property, not a fraction of budget.** MEASURED first-hand on this 4090 under `gpu_load.exe --gpu 0 --load 99` (nvidia-smi-confirmed 100% util, 2925–2940 MHz): dispatches of 0.262 / 0.322 / 0.362 / 0.400 ms took no penalty at all; at 5400 workgroups the same shader cost 5.653 ms. A bigger frame budget does not raise the cliff — it only gives a preempted dispatch somewhere to be preempted *to*.
2. **GPU duty is rate-invariant for a per-real-frame pass.** A pass costing *T* ms run *R* times per second eats *T·R*/1000 of the device no matter what the display does. 30 × 10.65 ms = 320 ms/s = **32% of the 4090**, before the game gets anything.
3. **The 0.84× baseline.** MEASURED, live BF6, operator playing, ~45 s paired runs, PhyriadFG at defaults, **no filter present**: multiplier 0.84×, present 89.4/s, iter 11.2/22.8 ms, latency 82.8 ms, consumed 47.9 of ~106 captured pairs/s. The only measured cure, `--gpu-priority realtime`, restores 2.74–2.78× at a cost of **18.6% of the game's fps** — above the project's own 10–15% band. There is no cheaper lever: `VK_EXT_global_priority_query` reports qfam 0 on this 4090 supports nothing above MEDIUM, and WDDM HIGH measured as a no-op (±0.03 multiplier across three paired runs).
4. **Capping our own output returns nothing** (§2.1, MEASURED). The only lever that frees GPU is capping the **game** — and what a capped game actually returns on this rig has never been measured either.
5. **The style-class ceiling.** A filter's natural range is styles defined by **subtraction** of information; styles whose identity is **added** information fail categorically. That is a property of the operator class, not of the budget, and it survives untouched.
6. **The packaging break.** A one-step distilled UNet is ~0.9–1.3B params ≈ 1.7–2.6 GB at FP16. `ui.exe` today embeds exactly one payload of 1,354,752 bytes via `include_bytes!`. A 1–2 GB payload is not a self-contained .exe in any meaningful sense.

### 2.4 The honest deflation: at 1080p the 8× is cancelled almost exactly

240 Hz → 30 fps is 7.999× more wall clock. 512×512 → 1920×1080 is 7.910× more pixels. **They cancel to within 1.1%** — and diffusion attention is superlinear in token count, so the true residual is *below* 1.0. Nothing that was dead at 1080p becomes alive at 1080p.

**What actually saved the diffusion tier is a different move that the 240 Hz framing never permitted: style small, upscale.** And that move is free here, which nobody had noticed: `fg_core_math.glsl:52-62` samples by **normalized uv** with `mv` divided by `out_size`, and the pipeline sampler is already `VK_FILTER_LINEAR` / `CLAMP_TO_EDGE` (`warp_blend.cpp:162`, verified first-hand). So a styled image bound at a *lower resolution* is upscaled by the warp's own hardware bilinear fetch with **zero** arithmetic change, zero extra dispatch, zero extra bandwidth. This matters because `use_upscale` is unconditionally forced false at runtime (`core_init.cpp:207-213`, verified: *"upscale: unsupported under the surface present path (v1) — forcing --no-upscale (the bridge blit scales)"*), so the two dedicated upscale shaders are structurally dead code.

### 2.5 THE DECIDING NUMBER, and what the existing data actually says about it

Every diffusion figure is uncontended. What a 10 ms dispatch costs with a game running is **unmeasured, and it is the highest-leverage unknown in the entire project.**

I re-read the raw sweep rather than the summary, and it is more informative than any of the underlying studies reported. Same 4-layer 64×64 head at 1080p, swept by tile size under `gpu_load --load 99 --heavy 4` (MEASURED):

| Workgroups | Implied idle time | Contended | Penalty (delta) | Penalty (ratio) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 5000 | 0.400 ms | 0.400 ms | 0.00 | 1.0× |
| 5400 | ~0.432 ms | **5.653 ms** | **+5.22** | 13.1× |
| 6480 | ~0.518 ms | **5.745 ms** | **+5.23** | 11.1× |
| 2-layer, full 1080p | 0.724 ms | 5.85 ms | +5.13 | 8.1× |
| 4-layer, full 1080p | 1.408 ms | 8.38 ms | +6.97 | 5.95× |
| 4-layer, `--heavy 1` | 1.408 ms | 3.66 ms | +2.25 | 2.6× |

**A 20% increase in work changed the delta by 0.006 ms (0.1%) while changing the ratio by 15%.** And the *same* 1.408 ms dispatch gives 3.66 ms against `--heavy 1` and 8.38 ms against `--heavy 4` — the penalty tracks the **competitor's** submission granularity, not our kernel's size. That is the signature of a fixed scheduling quantum being lost, not of a derating factor.

So there are **three** models, not two, and the three studies that addressed this disagreed because they each saw part of the picture:

| Model | Mechanism | An 11 ms style pass becomes | Fits 33.3 ms? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A — one lost quantum (additive)** | preempted once, +2 to +7 ms | 13–18 ms | **yes, comfortably** |
| **B — one quantum per quantum of duration** | preempted repeatedly as it spans competing submissions; penalty ∝ duration, slope ~1–1.8 | 22–31 ms | **yes, barely** |
| **C — constant ratio (multiplicative)** | 6–13× derating | 66–145 ms | **no, not even at 15 fps** |

**Model C is the one the data least supports** — the ratio demonstrably varies from 2.6× to 13.1× while the delta varies far less. But every measured dispatch is under 1.5 ms idle, i.e. shorter than roughly one quantum, so A and B are indistinguishable, and the extrapolation to 11 ms is **8× beyond the measured range**. That is not a small extrapolation.

There is also a fourth possibility raised by one study and worth taking seriously: **a diffusion UNet is not one dispatch.** It is hundreds of kernels, most individually well under 0.40 ms at 512×288. If the cliff is genuinely per-dispatch (as measured on a monolithic head), a UNet may slip under it entirely. If what actually matters is occupancy, it will not. **Untested, and cheap to test** — which is why Experiment 0 in §8 sweeps *both* a long monolithic dispatch and a many-small-dispatch pattern.

**One nuance that survives all four models:** the contention penalty is mostly *preemption latency*, not extra work. Our kernel still does ~11 ms of GPU work; it takes 14–31 ms of wall clock because it is descheduled. So the **deadline** question depends on the model, and the **duty** question does not: ~30 × 11 ms = ~33% of the device, whatever the model says.

## 3. THE MODEL SHORTLIST — LICENCE FIRST

### 3.1 Read this before the table

**Licence permissiveness and single-step latency are anti-correlated across the entire current field.** Everything fast enough to consider is licence-encumbered; everything cleanly licensed is 9× to 100× too slow. There is no clever route around this and no 2026 release fixed it.

Three disqualifications, loudly and early:

- **CreativeML Open RAIL-M / ++M weights cannot be bundled into an MIT release.** §4(a) verbatim: use-based restrictions *"MUST be included as an enforceable provision by You in any type of legal agreement ... governing the use and/or distribution of the Model or Derivatives"*. PhyriadFG's LICENSE grants rights *"without restriction"*. MIT cannot carry a use restriction. This disqualifies **SDXS, LCM / LCM-LoRA, Hyper-SD, SDXL-Lightning and PixArt** from being *bundled*; they can only travel alongside under their own terms, or be fetched at first run. *(Mitigating note: OpenRAIL's Attachment A contains **zero** IP-infringement clauses — it is a paperwork burden, not a constraint on what a user may prompt.)*
- **The best-measured single-4090 prompt-conditioned stylizer is not redistributable.** The 0.39B distilled edit UNet behind the 54.9 fps figure has weights *"gated by a safety-review / request process"*. The best number in the literature is attached to weights you cannot ship. (https://arxiv.org/html/2606.05981v1)
- **The NVIDIA route is foreclosed three independent ways** and no budget change touches any of them: no public NGX-NR API; the shipping DLL's 15 CUDA fatbins target sm_120 (Blackwell) only, so a 4090 cannot execute it as compiled; and RTX SDK licence §4(e) is incompatible with PhyriadFG's MIT grant.

### 3.3 The SD-Turbo licence disagreement — UNRESOLVED, and it is on the critical path

Two of the underlying studies contradicted each other, and I checked both artifacts myself today:

- **The HF tag on `stabilityai/sdxl-turbo` is `sai-nc-community`** (verified 2026-09-07).
- **The card text on both models says the opposite:** SD-Turbo — *"intended for both non-commercial and commercial usage ... For commercial use, please refer to https://stability.ai/license"*. SDXL-Turbo — *"intended for both non-commercial and commercial usage"*, pointing at stability.ai/membership (verified 2026-09-07).
- The Stability AI **Community License** (Last Updated July 5, 2024) permits commercial use and redistribution below **USD $1,000,000 annual revenue**, conditioned on registration at stability.ai/community-license, shipping a copy of the agreement, a NOTICE line, and a visible **"Powered by Stability AI"**, plus compliance with an external AUP that Stability can change unilaterally — and which, unlike OpenRAIL, **does** prohibit IP-infringing generation.

**Most likely reading: the tag is stale relative to the July 2024 licence update.** But *most likely* is not good enough to ship on. **Read `LICENSE.md` in the repo you actually download, on the day you download it, before you distribute anything.** This is a 20-minute task and it gates the whole shortlist.

### 3.4 The free deletion nobody should skip: precompute the prompt embeddings

He has **three presets**. A fixed prompt is a pure function evaluated once. Precompute three conditioning tensors offline and ship a few KB of floats. This deletes, in one move:

- **29.2 ms/frame** of text encoder from the best-measured pipeline's per-frame budget (~60% of it) — MEASURED at B=16 in arXiv 2606.05981;
- **680,820,392 bytes** of CLIP-L (SD-Turbo) MEASURED, or **5,288,159,306 bytes** of Gemma-2-2B-IT (SANA) MEASURED — *and Gemma's entire manually-gated, use-restricted licence surface with it*;
- the exact bottleneck the 2026 streaming-diffusion literature names: *"once the denoiser is aggressively distilled ... the text encoder becomes the per-frame critical path"*.

Cost: zero. Do it regardless of every other decision. **The consequence for the product: he does not ship a free text box, he ships named presets** — which §9 shows is also the right answer legally.

Second free deletion: swap the full VAE for **TAESD** (MIT). In the reference pipeline VAE encode + decode is 10.1 + 11.9 = **22 ms/frame** MEASURED; TAESD's decoder measures 6.4 ms/frame *on a smartphone SoC*, so on a 4090 it is well under 1 ms (ESTIMATE). Third: the 0.5–1.0 s end-to-end latency that made the 54.9 fps result a category error comes **entirely** from batching 8–16 frames. At batch 1 that batching is unnecessary and the latency penalty vanishes with it. **What remains is essentially the UNet step alone** — which is exactly what the 10.65 ms anchor measures.

### 3.5 The runtime, and the real deadline

**Prefer `stable-diffusion.cpp` on its Vulkan backend, and evaluate it before assuming CUDA.** MIT, pure C/C++, supports SD-Turbo img2img, **37.1 MB** Windows zip MEASURED (against 320.8 MB for the CUDA build and 537.3 MB with cudart), and — decisively — it runs on the **same Vulkan API PhyriadFG already uses**, which deletes the CUDA↔Vulkan interop entirely. That interop is MEASURED on this rig at 0.38 ms idle and **8.03 ms contended**, which is 24% of a 33.3 ms budget *for doing nothing*. The alternative is ONNX Runtime CUDA EP at **262–346 MB** MEASURED against a 1.29 MB `phyriad_fg.exe`, pinning CUDA 13.0 + cuDNN 9.x on the end user's machine.

**Its unknown is throughput: nobody has published a Vulkan-vs-TensorRT ratio for it.** That is the second measurement to make (§8).

**And the deadline is not 33.3 ms.** Two of the studies converged on this and it deflates the headline honestly:

- PhyriadFG's own iteration on live BF6 is 11.2 / 22.8 ms MEASURED, so the residual inside a 33.3 ms period is **10.5–22.1 ms** before the game receives any GPU at all;
- if the game keeps running at ~60 and captures stay at the MEASURED 59–61 pairs/s, the deadline is **16.4–16.9 ms**, not 33.3.

10.65 ms lands on the bottom edge of that residual with essentially no margin. *"Arithmetically alive with no margin"* is the correct sentence; *"8× of headroom"* is not.

## 4. HIS THREE PROMPTS

### 4.0 The general rule

**An img2img filter changes APPEARANCE, not CONTENT.** It repaints the pixels that are there. It will not change proportions, will not change hand topology, will not replace eye geometry, will not simplify a silhouette, and — because PhyriadFG warps by *measured* flow and cannot invent motion — it cannot touch **timing**. Re-drawing is generation, not filtering, and generation at 30 fps at any usable resolution does not exist on one consumer GPU.

The corollary, which four of the five studies reached independently from different premises: **the tool's natural range is styles defined by SUBTRACTION of information** (fewer colours, flattened gradients, suppressed texture). **Styles whose identity is ADDED information fail categorically** — not approximately. That convergence from independent directions is why I trust it more than any single argument in this document.

### 4.1 "Hiperrealista" — NO. Drop it from scope now rather than at the end.

**First, a factual correction:** this is **DLSS 5**, not DLSS 6. DLSS 5 *"infuses pixels with photoreal lighting and materials"*. DLSS 6 targets RTX 60/Rubin and real-time **generation** of game worlds, NPCs and quests; its demo slipped to CES 2027. And DLSS 5 has **no prompt input at all** — it consumes only colour + motion vectors + carried temporal state + scalar artistic values.

Five independent reasons this is the worst possible target, any one of which is sufficient:

1. **Information.** DLSS 5's advantage is *training-time* supervision from renderer-derived scene attributes (albedo, normals, lighting) that grounds generation to the authored scene. PhyriadFG has the finished, tonemapped, HUD-composited, motion-blurred image. An open img2img model sees colour only and invents pores, weave and brick from priors with **no scene basis**.
2. **Temporal.** Because the invented detail has no scene referent, it is resampled independently each style op and boils on exactly the surfaces the eye is inspecting for realism — on top of a warp whose presented positional error is 1.668 px MEASURED at low phase, biased forward along motion.
3. **Resolution — and this one is fatal on its own.** The only affordable configuration is 512×512 or smaller upscaled 2.7–3.75×. Hyperrealism means *adding high-frequency detail and then throwing it away in the upscale*. Self-defeating by construction, independent of model quality.
4. **Error visibility.** Human vision carries a trained high-precision prior for faces, hands, skin, text and straight architecture. Identical model error reads as **wrong** in the realism direction and as **style** in the cartoon direction. That is the uncanny valley, and it is not tunable.
5. **The HUD.** Capture is post-composite and there is no UI mask. It will stylize the crosshair, health bars, subtitles and minimap. DLSS 5 solves this only with developer-authored engine masks a capture-based overlay can never obtain.

**And the competitive fact:** DLSS 5 already does this natively — 148M params FP8, one step, driver-integrated — and its own cost is brutal exactly as he says (RTX 5070 Ti, 1440p, Quality DLSS: **68.9 fps avg / 52.3 fps 1% low** with neural rendering on, versus ~240 / 168.2 off — MEASURED third-party). DLSS 5 is also RTX 50-only with no RTX 40 path, so on this 4090 it is not a comparison, it is a different product tier. Competing with a shipped competitor doing the same job better, at ~6 ms of stolen GPU, is not a scope.

**What DOES land, and he should be offered it explicitly:** a **photographic grade** — film-emulation LUT, filmic tonemap, bloom, chromatic aberration, static-pattern grain, vignette, edge sharpen. 0.02–0.20 ms at **native 1080p** (ESTIMATE), and a per-pixel operator commutes exactly with the warp so it is structurally incapable of flickering. Many players will read it as "more cinematic". **The limit must be stated plainly: it re-tones, it never relights.** "Golden hour" gives golden-hour *colour*, never golden-hour *shadows*. Selling that as "hiperrealista" is honest only with that sentence attached.

### 4.2 "Estilo Looney Tunes" — lands as a palette, fails as a style.

Rubber-hose identity is **squash-and-stretch, timing, smear frames, wildly varying tapered ink weight, and 1930s film artifacts**. Deformation and timing are *motion* properties. PhyriadFG warps rather than redraws, and a per-frame image diffuser has no timing at all. Its palette and line are not distinctive enough to carry the style alone.

Worse, it fails a second way that matters here: a model genuinely attempting rubber-hose must **move pixels**, which destroys the correspondence the warp assumes, so propagation stops working and every frame becomes an independent sample — **maximum strobe.**

**Honest deliverable: "a Looney-Tunes-*coloured* game."** Which will read as much closer to Simpsons than to Looney Tunes, and therefore as a failure rather than a near miss.

### 4.3 "Estilo Simpsons" — the best of the three, and the demo. With two stings.

Its identity is unusually reachable because it is almost entirely **subtraction**: a ~12-colour palette, flat fills with no gradients, hard uniform-weight black outlines, no texture, no cast shadows. Those four things survive a 3× upscale nearly intact (flat regions are the lowest-spatial-frequency content that exists, and are exactly what bilinear preserves). And in an FPS the screen is mostly *environment*, where those four things carry most of the look — so **Simpsons-on-environments will land visibly better than Simpsons-on-characters.**

**Sting one — the honest label is "Simpsons palette and ink", not "Simpsons".** Round yellow heads, four fingers, large overlapping white eye circles, a head at 1/4 body height instead of 1/7.5 — that is character design, i.e. content. No filter of any class produces it.

**Sting two, and it is the uncomfortable one: the colour half is reachable without diffusion at all.** A 3D LUT plus an edge threshold is 0.10–0.20 ms at native 1080p (ESTIMATE), or 0.30–0.55 ms with a separable bilateral for tone flattening — **under the MEASURED 0.40 ms cliff, at 1080p, with no model, no multi-GB payload, no CUDA dependency and no per-machine engine build.** So the diffusion tier must beat a free alternative on precisely the prompt where it looks best.

And the low-strength trap makes it worse: at denoising strength 0.15–0.30 — the only band where diffusion is temporally *safe* — what it produces **is** a grade plus a mild flatten, i.e. exactly what the free ladder delivers. At 0.5+ it looks genuinely different and it boils. **Diffusion only earns its cost in the narrow 0.35–0.55 band, and only if the boiling is solved.**

### 4.4 The ranking, and the band he did not name

**Estilo Simpsons > Estilo Looney Tunes >> Hiperrealista.** Note that this ranking is the *opposite* of what the DLSS 5 framing suggests: the prompt he was most excited about is structurally the worst possible ask for this tool, and the one that works best is the one that barely needs the model.

**Diffusion's real value sits in a band he did not name:** painterly, ink-wash, comic-with-hatching, cross-processed film, halftone — styles that are neither pure subtraction (free without diffusion) nor pure addition (broken with it). If he pursues the neural tier, that is where it should be aimed.

**Prompt vocabulary, ranked — and note the inversion, which should drive the UI:** palette/grade prompts ("noir", "sepia", "bleach bypass", "synthwave", "thermal") land essentially fully at <0.05 ms; lighting-mood prompts land mostly; cartoon-with-outlines prompts land partially and are the best case for neural spend; medium/texture prompts ("watercolour", "oil", "pencil", "pixel art", "papercraft") fail categorically; **named-IP prompts are technically worst and legally worst simultaneously — and they are the ones a user types first and judges the product by.**

## 5. THE ARCHITECTURE — "stylize slow, frame-generate fast"

### 5.1 The mechanism holds. I verified it in the generated code, not the docs.

`build/gen/shaders/chain_compose.glsl` is four lines: `c_out = core.color` → `select` (260) → `stasis` (280) → `single_track` (290). `layer_manifest.txt` confirms 28 rows with `COMPOSE 290 single_track default=1 overrides=1`. And `shaders/layers/single_track.glsl` (read in full, verbatim):

```glsl
vec4 pfg_compose_single_track(vec4 c_in, in LayerCtx ctx) {   // OVERRIDE: c_in is deliberately discarded
    vec4 r = ctx.B_samp;
    if (SINGLE_TRACK_no_stasis == 0u) {
        const vec4  cur0   = texture(u_cur_real, ctx.uv);
        const float d_zero = length(cur0.rgb - texture(u_prev_real, ctx.uv).rgb);
        float w_s = smoothstep(1.2, 3.0, (ctx.d_pixel + 0.02) / (d_zero + 0.02));
        if (ctx.stasis_block) w_s = 1.0;
        r = mix(ctx.B_samp, cur0, w_s);
    }
    return r;
}
```

`B_samp = texture(u_cur_real, uv + inv_t_mv_uv)` (`fg_core_math.glsl:52-62`). **Both mix terms sample `u_cur_real`.** `u_prev_real` contributes only to a scalar weight. **CONFIRMED: one styled image per pair styles 100% of the presented colour output, exactly.** Measured cadence 240 ticks/s against 59–61 captured pairs/s — the warp resamples it every tick for free.

Two consequences worth stating plainly:
- **FILTER_VERDICT's standing objection that "real and generated frames would stylize differently and the detail would strobe at the real-frame rate" does NOT apply in this configuration** — no unstyled real frame is ever presented. That objection should be retired for the shipping default.
- **The optical flow is computed on the UNSTYLED frames, structurally and for free.** Thread F uploads `hR_b` into its own Bframe views; thread P uploads `hR_a` into `wapPrevA`/`wapCurA`. A P-side style pass physically cannot reach flow. This kills the "cel-flattened frames are the aperture problem everywhere and would wreck the block matcher" objection: **the matcher never sees them.** It also sidesteps a real decalibration hazard (SAD statistics, the GME affine residual scale, `kChangeGateSadZ`, stasis thresholds are all calibrated on real-content contrast).

### 5.2 The plumbing is genuinely as cheap as claimed — I checked the descriptor table

`fgcore_create` declares `const VkDescriptorSetLayoutBinding bd[15]` covering bindings **0..13 plus 15** (the LayerParams UBO), with `bindingCount=15`. **There is no binding 14.** Total change: one image, one `VkDescriptorSetLayoutBinding`, one descriptor write, `COMBINED_IMAGE_SAMPLER` pool size 11 → 12, +2 texture fetches in a shader already doing ~20, and one `PFG_LAYER` row. Off is a specialization constant folded at pipeline creation, so byte-identical when disabled.

**One correction to FILTER_PLAN's own six-edit recipe, and it matters.** Its item (3) says to swap `fg_sample`'s `cur` argument to the styled sampler. **Do not.** That silently makes `ctx.d_pixel` a styled-domain quantity while `d_zero` (correctly) stays unstyled, so the ratio `(d_pixel+0.02)/(d_zero+0.02)` compares two different photometric domains through a hard-coded `smoothstep(1.2, 3.0)`. That ratio **is** `w_s` — the screen-static evidence mix, i.e. the HUD protection, which is the best-behaved measured class in the whole baseline (0.046 px over a 120 px/s pan). A posterising style flattens regions, drives `d_pixel` toward 0, drags `w_s` toward 0, and changes which MV the warp uses (`bg_reclaim`, default ON at 4.0, *rewrites* the primary MV). **Correct fix: leave `fg_sample` entirely on the unstyled pair and take two extra taps on binding 14 for content only.** Evidence path and both gates then byte-identical.

### 5.3 Judged strictly: the stated differentiator is the weaker half

**Is it genuinely differentiating? Partly — but not for the reason he gave.**

- **"The frame generator hides the stylizer's low rate"** is true but shallow. Anyone can hide a low rate with off-the-shelf RIFE or a DXGI-level frame generator. And it hides the **rate** while *concentrating the **inconsistency*** (see §6). This half is not a moat.
- **What nobody else has:** PhyriadFG computes, on the GPU, per pair, as part of its shipping path, the **dense bidirectional flow field with dissidence masks** that every published streaming-stylization system needs as its temporal-consistency conditioning and pays dearly for. In the 2026 streaming-stylization paper, CPU Farnebäck flow was **~848 ms of an 882 ms latency spike (96%)**, requiring 8 worker threads plus async prefetch to bring p95 from 1439 ms to 756 ms. PhyriadFG gets it free, forward *and* backward, GPU-resident.

**And there is an exact structural alignment that is the single strongest technical asset in the project:** PhyriadFG's MV grid is `ceil(W/8) × ceil(H/8)` — enforced by a runtime FATAL assert at `flow_init.cpp:52-56` — which at 1080p with `flow_div=1` is **240 × 135**. The SD 1.x/2.x/XL VAE downsamples spatially by exactly **8×**, so the latent for a 1920×1080 image is **240 × 135**. *One motion vector per latent cell, zero resampling, and it holds at every resolution because both are /8.* The published state of the art (Go-with-the-Flow) computes flow in image space and mean-pools it down to reach this grid; PhyriadFG is already on it natively. The flow is also far finer than the latent can represent: 1.668 px = 0.209 latent cells on the default, 0.271 px = 0.034 cells with `--no-mv-guided`.

**The redirect I would actually recommend: warp the PREVIOUS styled frame by that field and feed it to the model as its img2img initialization.** That is the published cure for the flicker, applied at its source instead of hidden, using the one expensive ingredient he already has. It is safe under the existing rule — *"do not build a feedback path"* is about feeding styled frames into the **flow**, and flow stays structurally immune.

**Two costs to state honestly against it.** (a) `--flow-scale 2`, the only lever MEASURED to actually return GPU (iter 2.73 → 0.94 ms, util 95% → 56%, power 294 → 138 W, AddedLatency 43.7 → 13.2 ms, at `gme_dissidence` 0.02 → 0.15), **halves the MV grid to 120×68 and breaks the exact latent alignment.** That conflict is not noted in any existing document and it forces a deliberate choice. (b) Warping *latents* by RGB-space flow is an approximation — VAE latent channels are not spatially localised in a simple way. Rerender-A-Video does it anyway; Go-with-the-Flow deliberately chose image space instead. Unresolved.

### 5.4 Two structural breaks that the reframe does not repair

**Break 1 — the style rate and the capture rate must be the same number.** Under `single_track` the output within a pair is 100% a function of that pair's `cur`. If you capture at 60 and style every Nth frame, then for the N−1 unstyled pairs you either present unstyled — **the style strobes on and off, catastrophic** — or you chain warps from a stale styled frame, compounding positional bias and adding a bilinear resample per link (a path that does not exist in the code). **So capture must be decimated to the style rate.** That is not free: it multiplies per-pair displacement by exactly the decimation factor.

**And that break lands on a measured wall.** The matcher's coarse reach is **±48 px per pair** at `flow_div=1` (a documented design fact), and MEASURED degradation begins beyond **~12–16 px per pair**: 5.2 px error with **29 of 96 markers simply ABSENT**, both runs agreeing at r = 1.00. The wall is in px-per-*pair*, so halving the capture rate halves the on-screen speed at which it triggers. A modest 400 px/s pan is 6.7 px/pair at 60 fps capture, **13.3 at 30 (right at the wall)**, and 26.7 at 15 (well past it).

> **The 15 fps branch of the proposal is dead on a measured number. 30 fps capture is the floor, and it is marginal.**

**Break 2 — at 30 fps OUTPUT the frame generator has nothing to generate.** Multiplier 1.0, and he would pay PhyriadFG's MEASURED 13–83 ms of added latency for zero benefit. **The reframe that saves it: read "30 fps" as the STYLE rate, not the output rate.** Cap the game near 30, style once per real frame, present at 60–240 Hz through the existing warp. On his 240 Hz panel every candidate rate divides evenly (240/30 = 8, /24 = 10, /20 = 12, /15 = 16), so none of them introduces cadence judder **on this display** — the judder objection to low rates does not apply to his rig.

### 5.5 The shape that survives

**Capture = style = ~30 fps (never 15). Style at 512×288 or 512×512. Present at 60–120 Hz. Warped-previous-styled-frame feedback into the model. Guidance rows on the unstyled reals at bindings 0/1; styled content on binding 14; `fg_sample` untouched. Flat-cartoon-class prompts only. Game capped by the user. Slow single-player titles only.**

**Two things that are NOT constraints and should stop being budgeted.**
- **VRAM.** The ~598 MB capture ring is **host** memory — `_aligned_malloc` + `hbuf_import` via `VK_EXT_external_memory_host` (`core_init.cpp:431-439`); BENCHMARKS §6 says it in terms: *"the capture ring holds real frames in system RAM."* PhyriadFG's actual VRAM is under ~100 MB. Against 24 GB minus a game's 8–12 GB, an SD1.5 stack at 1.5–3.5 GB fits with room to spare. Cap at 6–8 GB only because overcommitting makes WDDM evict the **game's** resources — a far worse failure than any frame-rate cost. **Time is the wall, not VRAM.**
- **Upscaling.** 540p → 1080p costs 0.02–0.04 ms with the bilinear blit that is already live — noise against 11 ms. **A better upscaler is not warranted; the right fix is structural:** run the **colour** through the model at low resolution and upsample it (flat regions are exactly what bilinear preserves), and compute the **line** at native 1080p from the Sobel field the codebase already produces. Colour is low-frequency, line is high-frequency; upsampling the low half is free and upsampling the high half is the only thing that would look bad.

## 6. TEMPORAL COHERENCE — does it look stylized, or does it look like flickering AI?

### 6.1 The honest answer

**At 30 style ops/s with the best affordable stabilizer, "hiperrealista" and anything painterly will look like a flickering AI video — specifically like Deforum / WarpFusion output**, which is a named look with a large public corpus he can go and look at today. That is not a generic worry; every reason is a measured property of *this* codebase:

1. **Disocclusion is UNHANDLED on the default path.** Verified first-hand: the entire fwd/bwd round-trip classification, fill-div, matte, background-snap and disocclusion-commit block is gated at `wap_warp.comp:697-701` on `occl_thresh > 0.0`, and `occl_thresh` defaults to **0** (*"0 = bidirectional flow OFF (DEFAULT)"*, `wap_warp.comp:85`). The generated `layer_manifest.txt` lists all 28 rows and contains **no occlusion row**. And `warp_ok` (Gate 1 confidence AND Gate 2 tile agreement) is computed and then thrown away — it feeds only `select` at rank 260, whose output the rank-290 override discards (`select.glsl:10`). **So every disocclusion hole is filled by the next independent stylization — a detail pop at the style rate, at the silhouette of everything that moves. That is exactly the mechanism that produces the WarpFusion look.**
2. **Thin geometry is below the flow's resolution floor.** A cartoon ink line is 1–2 px; the MV tile is 8×8 px. The same field is 4–8× too coarse for an outline while being 5–30× finer than a latent needs.
3. **Fast camera walks into the measured 12–16 px/pair wall**, and 30 fps capture doubles the on-screen speed at which it triggers.
4. **The shipping default costs 6× the presented error.** MEASURED, four-condition table, two independent 16-triple captures: default **1.668 px** at low phase; `--st-no-stasis` 1.693; `--no-mv-guided --mv-median` 1.493; **`--no-mv-guided` 0.271 px, flat across phase** (per-phase 0.271/0.246/0.321/0.301; runs 0.267/0.301). Raw MV endpoint error is 0.464 px mean / 0.180 median independently — **the flow is good; the default layer stack on top of it costs the 1.4 px.**
5. **The HUD gets styled**, and no conditioning mechanism at any strength can protect text. This is arithmetic: SD's VAE downsamples 8×, so styling 1080p at 512 px wide makes one latent cell cover 30 px of output; a 16–24 px glyph is **0.53–0.80 of ONE latent cell** — below the representable resolution of the space the model works in.

### 6.2 The correction that matters most for the cartoon case

**The famous 1.668 px is not the warp's accuracy — it is one default-on layer's artifact, and the cure is a flag he already owns.** For a filter drawing 1–2 px ink lines, 1.668 px means the outline walks off its edge every pair; 0.271 px means it stays put. `--no-mv-consensus` / `--no-mv-guided` (FLOW rank 90 exists precisely because of this finding) is already on his open list; **the filter work makes it load-bearing rather than optional.**

**The catch:** `--no-mv-guided` also disables the colour-guided corner pick, which is what keeps MVs correct at object *rims* — exactly where a cartoon puts its ink. **The two cures fight, and nobody has measured which wins.**

### 6.3 The counterintuitive finding, and the most actionable line in this document

**Dropping to 30 fps makes the strobe WORSE on two independent axes at once, even while it makes the budget 8× better.**

- **Perceptual:** because the output within a pair is 100% a function of that pair's `cur`, the style is frozen and advected *within* a pair and all boiling happens at **pair boundaries**. So the strobe frequency **is** the style rate. Human flicker sensitivity is bandpass and peaks at **10–20 Hz**. A 30 Hz style rate sits at/near that peak; the shipping ~60 Hz pair rate sits above it. Worse, at the boundary the *geometry* is continuous while the *appearance* steps — decoupling smooth tracked motion from a stepped appearance makes the pop **more** visible, not less, and the eye integrates a full-frame pop while ignoring scattered shimmer.
- **Geometric:** halving the capture rate doubles per-pair displacement into the MEASURED 12–16 px/pair wall (§5.4).

> **Therefore: buy GPU budget from RESOLUTION, never from the style rate.** Styling at ~60 Hz on a 512×288 base is perceptually better than styling at 30 Hz on a 720p base, for the same GPU-seconds. Cutting resolution costs blur, which the warp advects smoothly and which a stylized look partly hides; cutting the style rate costs strobe, which is the failure mode under study.

**And here is the bind, stated plainly because it is the least comfortable synthesis in this study:** 60 styled frames/s at 512×512 is 660 ms/s = **66% of the device**, which is not affordable. So the budget *forces* him to 30, which is the configuration that is worst for temporal quality. He does not get to choose both.

### 6.4 The verdict is style-dependent, and the axis is the same one as §4

Per-frame diffusion is a many-to-one map from (noise, conditioning) to image: a different noise sample gives a different **high-frequency** field but converges to the same low-frequency structure. **A style whose identity is reduction of information has almost nothing to disagree about between seeds. A style whose identity is added information is made ENTIRELY of the part that changes with the seed.** Flat Simpsons-class fills strobe least. Hyperrealism strobes most. That is measurable in an afternoon (§8, E2) and it is the cheapest quality experiment available.

### 6.5 Two fixes that are free, and two that are out

| Fix | Cost | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| **Fixed/shared seed** | zero (a constant instead of an RNG call) | **In.** But state the failure mode: the noise is **screen-locked**, so under camera motion the detail field stays pinned to the screen while the scene slides beneath it — it trades boiling for a fixed grain/dirty-lens layer. A different artifact, not none. |
| **Latent warping by the existing MV field** | <0.005 ms ESTIMATE (240×135×4 fp16 = 259 KB, 1/64 the texels of the full-res warp batch which measures 0.08–0.11 ms) | **In.** Exact grid alignment, sub-cell flow error, ~80× under the cliff. |
| **Keyframe + propagate** | already built | **In.** This *is* the architecture. For scale: Rerender-A-Video, the canonical published instance, measures 14.23 s/keyframe and 1.49 s/non-keyframe at 512×512 on a Tesla V100; PhyriadFG's propagation is 0.08–0.11 ms MEASURED at 1080p. Different hardware, algorithm and resolution — a ratio between two measurements under different conditions, not a controlled comparison — but the order of magnitude is the point. Its natural K (~4) is the same regime Rerender chose by hand (K=10). |
| Cross-frame attention (StreamV2V) | 20 fps on a single **A100**, resolution not stated | **Out.** Datacentre GPU, and attention over a cached bank is the most expensive part of the model. |
| Rolling KV cache (StreamDiffusionV2, RTR-DiT) | 24 fps at 512×512 on **FOUR 4090s**; RTR-DiT on an H20 | **Out.** Architecture features of autoregressive video DiTs, not bolt-ons to an img2img checkpoint. |
| **Warped noise (Go-with-the-Flow)** | algorithm is cheap: 2.14 ms MEASURED at 1024² on an A100, ~<0.1 ms ESTIMATE at 240×135 | **Blocked, and this is the biggest open question in the study.** It works by pre-processing training videos into structured noise and **fine-tuning on it**. Released checkpoints are CogVideoX/SVD video models, not one-step img2img stylizers. Every model he could run at 30 Hz was trained on i.i.d. noise. **Feeding warped noise to a checkpoint not trained for it is untested and I found no measurement of it.** |

### 6.6 Scene cuts — free, and one rule nobody publishes

The cheapest reliable detector needs **zero GPU work, zero new buffers and zero new passes**: three signals already decoded per pair inside `gme_fit_affine`'s existing loop (which already walks the whole fp16 MV grid and the whole `sad_zero` grid into thread-local scratch every pair) — (a) mean `sad_zero` saturates, (b) mean `sad_best` / mean `sad_zero` → 1, (c) GME dissidence already high. ~32,400 float accumulations per pair, sub-microsecond class (ESTIMATE, unmeasured). Latch by reusing `--motion-fallback` verbatim (EMA α=0.2, Schmitt, `kMfHyst=12.0`, `kMfDwell=4`) — already tuned against flapping. Corroborate with a luminance-histogram delta, which is exactly what NVIDIA's own community wrapper uses for its history-reset flag.

Four rules on a cut. Two are not in any existing document:
1. **Drop warp propagation for that pair** — a warp across a cut is garbage by construction.
2. **Force a fresh stylization; do not carry the previous latent or warped noise** — that imports the old scene's structure into the new one.
3. **Do NOT crossfade the style. Cut hard, matching the content cut.** A style dissolve is a whole-screen simultaneous change, which is perceptually worse than local shimmer.
4. **On the STATIC side of a cut — menus, loading, pause, inventory — SHED the filter and HOLD the last styled frame.** No research test set contains a pause menu, so nobody covers this, and it is the worst case for a stylizer: static content means no flow to propagate, so every styled frame is independently sampled, so boiling is **maximal** — on the most-looked-at, longest-dwelt content in the game. Holding is free, and the same signal that detects the cut (mean `sad_zero` → 0) detects the staticness.

## 7. WHAT THIS COSTS THE GAME

### 7.1 Plainly: this is a LOOK tool that SPENDS performance, not a performance tool that saves it.

And the escape hatch is already **measured shut**. Capping PhyriadFG's own output from 240 to 120 on live BF6 returned nothing to the game (arrival 83 → 79 fps, gpu-A 91% → 88%) and made the latency floor **worse** (min 14.5 → 18.5 ms) — MEASURED, paired runs, corroborated by the operator's own in-game counter. Root cause on record: the saturation tax is capture-side and scales with the **game's** frame rate. **The only lever that frees GPU is capping the GAME** — and what a capped game actually returns on this rig has never been measured either (§8, E1b).

### 7.2 The bill

**GPU duty at 30 styled frames/s** (ESTIMATE, area-scaled from the MEASURED 10.65 ms anchor; linear-in-pixels, which is optimistic below 512×512 because fixed dispatch overhead and 128 idle SMs do not shrink, and pessimistic above it because attention is quadratic in tokens; **a hard floor of ~3–4 ms/step exists regardless of how small you go**):

| Style resolution | Upscale to 1080p | ms/step | Duty @ 30/s | Duty @ 15/s |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 512×288 | 3.75× | ~5.99 | **18%** | 9% |
| 640×360 | 3.00× | ~9.36 | **28%** | 14% |
| **512×512 (the MEASURED anchor)** | 3.75× (aspect-corrected) | **10.65 MEASURED** | **32%** | 16% |
| 768×432 | 2.50× | ~13.48 | 40% | 20% |
| 960×540 | 2.00× | ~21.07 | 63% | 32% |
| 1920×1080 | 1× | ~84.24 | **253% — impossible** | 126% |

**Note the resolution lever is smaller than it looks between neighbours:** 640×360 is 0.879× of 512×512's pixel count, so it buys ~12%, not a fraction. The lever pays between 512×512 and 1080p and is nearly exhausted below it. **Do not plan around shrinking to 360p.**

**Latency, honestly composed** (ESTIMATE built on MEASURED inputs):

| Term | Value | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Game-side e2e at ~30 fps | ~50–70 ms | ESTIMATE |
| PhyriadFG's own added latency | **13.2 ms** (flow-scale 2, light game) / **43.7 ms** (flow-scale 1, light game) / **82.8 ms** (live BF6 defaults) / **19.5 ms** (BF6 + `--gpu-priority realtime`) | **MEASURED** |
| Style pass + one styled-frame period of staleness | ~33 ms staleness + 14–31 ms pass | ESTIMATE |
| **Total, slow-game configuration** | **~110–145 ms** | ESTIMATE |
| **Total, shooter configuration** | **~150–190 ms** | ESTIMATE |

Against a ~100 ms external playability line, a ~15 ms expert-FPS perception threshold, and a sub-50 ms competitive target. SIGGRAPH Asia 2019 measured that 30 ms of latency reduction benefits first-person targeting *more* than refresh rate above 60 Hz. **The slow-game figure is at the line. The shooter figure is not a shooter configuration.**

### 7.3 What he would actually experience

- **Slow single-player game, capped near 30:** ~110–145 ms total added latency, with a stylized image presented smoothly at 60–120 Hz. **That works, and it is the demo.**
- **BF6:** no. The tool already measures 0.84× there with nothing added, and 150–190 ms is not a shooter. **Adding a 32% duty pass to a deficit is not a tuning problem; it is the same problem the project already failed to solve.**

### 7.4 What that means for how it ships

1. **Ship it as an opt-in styled MODE, off by default, clearly labelled as costing frames.** Not as a feature of the frame generator.
2. **Ship it with an explicit "cap your game" instruction**, because that is the only measured lever, and say so.
3. **Ship a whitelist/blacklist by title class**, or at minimum a startup warning when the captured rate implies a GPU-heavy title.
4. **The single self-contained .exe dies for the styled mode, and this is no longer a product-identity question he gets to defer — the architecture decides it.** `ui.exe` embeds one payload of 1,354,752 bytes today; an SD1.5 FP8 TensorRT engine is ~1 GB, and a TensorRT engine must additionally be **built on the target machine for its exact GPU and driver** (engines are not portable across architectures without hardware-compatibility mode; any TRT upgrade invalidates the cache), which is a multi-minute first-run step. **Fetch the weights at first run from the licensor's own host** — that also removes most of the redistribution obligations in both the OpenRAIL and Stability families, since those attach to *distributing* the model (medium confidence: this is the standard reading and standard practice, not a clause that says so).
5. **The cheap tiers have none of this.** A coopmat net is ~200 KB per style, and `CMakeLists.txt:48-65` already embeds every compiled SPIR-V blob as a `constexpr` array. **Ship the non-neural ladder regardless — it is under 2% of the GPU at 30 fps, works at native 1080p, and is the honest floor the neural tier has to beat.**

## 8. THE FIRST EXPERIMENT — days, not weeks

**Stop at the first failure. Do not write a line of integration C++ until E0 and E1 have both passed.**

### E0 — Settle additive vs multiplicative. **One afternoon. Decides everything.**

The instrument already exists (`cmbench.exe` + `mlp1080*.spv` against `tools/gpu_load.exe`, plus the probe in the scratchpad directory).

**Do:** sweep the cooperative-matrix probe's layer count to 8 / 16 / 32 / 64 (≈3 / 6 / 12 / 24 ms idle) against the same 100% saturation source, **and repeat it with a many-small-dispatch pattern** (dozens of sub-0.4 ms dispatches summing to the same total), **and repeat both against a real game instead of `gpu_load.exe`.** Plot penalty (contended − idle) against idle time.

**Read it:**
- **Flat line** (penalty stays 2–7 ms) → **Model A. The project is alive** and you have the number every conclusion in this document rests on.
- **Slope ≈ 1–1.8** → **Model B. Still alive, barely** — an 11 ms pass becomes 22–31 ms, which fits 33.3 ms with no margin. Re-plan at 512×288 only.
- **Constant ratio 6–13×** → **Model C. STOP. Diffusion is dead at 30 fps too**, and the honest answer is the cooperative-matrix tier, which the 8× budget promotes from exactly 1 layer to 4+ anyway.
- **Many-small beats one-big by a wide margin** → the cliff does not apply to a UNet the way it applies to a monolithic head, and every estimate here is conservative.

**STOP RULE: if the curve is Model C, stop the diffusion track entirely and do not spend another day on it.**

### E1 — The one number that does not exist in the literature. **One afternoon, in parallel.**

Run a free one-step img2img model (SD-Turbo via `stable-diffusion.cpp` on **Vulkan**, and via TensorRT if you have it) at **batch 1** on a captured 512×512 frame, **first idle, then with BF6 in the foreground**, and read the wall clock. The three published anchors for "one styled 512×512 frame on one 4090" span **16×** (10.65 ms / 18.2 ms at batch 8–16 / 167 ms implied by StreamDiffusionV2's four-GPU figure) and **none of them is batch-1-with-contention**.

**Also measure the Vulkan-vs-TensorRT ratio** while you are there — that single ratio decides whether the clean 37.1 MB MIT same-API route is viable or whether the only viable route is the 262–346 MB CUDA one with its version pinning. I found user anecdotes and no benchmark at all.

**STOP RULE: if a batch-1 512×512 step lands over ~35 ms with a game running, the whole thing is a screenshot mode and should be shipped as one, honestly.** Under ~15 ms, build the redirect in §5.5.

### E1b — Re-measure the current default binary, and measure a capped game. **Same afternoon.**

Every utilization, power and `iter_ms` figure used in this document is from **2026-06-22** and predates both R7(a) (`fg_core.comp` as default, 2026-09-06) and the R4 present-stage extraction (2026-09-05). And the "a game capped to 30 frees ~70% of the 4090" chain is an extrapolation from BF6 at 99% uncapped that has **never been measured** — engines do not scale utilization linearly with a cap. **If a capped BF6 still sits at 60%+, the style budget halves and §7.2's table is wrong.**

### E2 — Look at the picture, before any C++. **One day.**

`--qdump` 40 frames of real gameplay. Then, entirely offline in Python:

- **(a) The seed-variance probe.** Stylize each frame **twice** with the same model and prompt but different seeds. 1. **Contention: additive vs multiplicative.** Two studies read the same table opposite ways. **Resolved as far as the data allows** by re-reading the raw sweep: at 5400 and 6480 workgroups the *delta* is constant to 0.006 ms while the *ratio* moves 15%, which strongly disfavours the multiplicative model. ## 9. IP PRACTICES

Style itself is not the problem — **artistic style is unprotectable in the US** (idea/expression, 17 U.S.C. §102(b); the Copyright Office states copyright *"is not affected by style or form"*), and in the EU a style cannot be a "work" at all (Cofemel C-683/17: an *"aesthetic effect"* is *"the result of subjective sensation"* and fails the required precision-and-objectivity test). Tellingly, both studios currently suing Midjourney over exactly his two cartoon examples — Disney over Bart Simpson (C.D. Cal. 2:25-cv-05275) and Warner over Bugs Bunny (filed 2025-09-04) — plead **character reproduction**, not style copying. **The risk is not the look; it is the NAME and the INPUT.** Three practices, each cheap: **(1) Ship a prompt box and zero presets named after a property.** Under *Cox v. Sony* (No. 24-171, decided 2026-03-25) a tool with substantial noninfringing uses whose developer performs no *"specific act"* of encouragement is not contributorily liable, and *"mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient"* — an empty text field is exactly that, while a button labelled "Simpsons" is the developer choosing to write the word, and is the *Grokster* fact pattern. Name presets by **mechanism** the way Adobe Firefly does (~130 presets, **zero** named after any artist, studio or brand), name public-domain sources freely the way Photoshop names Van Gogh and Monet, and extend the rule to the marketing surface — a demo video titled "PhyriadFG Simpsons mode" is the same inducement fact as the preset. Naming the **games** he supports is fine and always was (nominative fair use). **(2) Never bundle a reference image.** A text preset is a string literal; an image-conditioned preset (reference frame, IP-Adapter conditioning, or an embedding derived from frames) puts actual copies of studio frames inside the .exe — that is direct reproduction under §106, with no style defence and no *Cox* protection. Since he cannot train anyway, this reduces to one flat rule: **no copyrighted frames in the distribution, in any form.** Note that this is the same conclusion §3.4 reaches for free on performance grounds — three precomputed prompt embeddings, no reference images, no text encoder. **(3) One README line and the right licence:** *"PhyriadFG generates images from text you supply. You are responsible for what you prompt and for any use of the result. Do not use it to reproduce characters or works you do not own or have rights to."* — *Cox* expressly credited the defendant for having *"repeatedly discouraged copyright infringement."* On licences: Apache-2.0 (FLUX.1-schnell, SANA) is ship-and-forget; **FLUX.1 [dev] is out the moment any money moves, including a donate button**; OpenRAIL costs an EULA paragraph but constrains nothing about prompting; Stability costs a NOTICE file, a visible *"Powered by Stability AI"*, registration, and an external AUP that *does* prohibit IP-infringing generation and that Stability can change without asking. **Two caveats: I am not a lawyer, and this is US + EU only — Mexican law and choice-of-law for a globally-downloaded .exe published from Mexico were not researched, and practical exposure for a free hobby tool is likely dominated by platform takedowns rather than litigation. One date to watch: the *Andersen v. Stability AI* jury trial begins 2026-09-08 — tomorrow — and it is the first to put the "published list of style names" theory to a jury.**

1. **The reframe is correct and it genuinely moves things** — a full 512×512 one-step pipeline goes from 256% of the budget to 32%. Things that were dead ARE now alive.

**Recommendation:** THE ARITHMETIC, HONESTLY REDONE. The operator's reframe is correct and it genuinely moves things: at 33.3 ms instead of 4.167 ms, a full 512x512 one-step diffusion pipeline costs ~11-12 ms uncontended, which is 35% of the budget instead of 256% of it. Things that were dead ARE now alive. But three corrections must go with that.

FIRST CORRECTION: the resolution lever is smaller than it looks. 640x360 is 0.879x of 512x512's pixels, so it buys 12%, not a fraction. The law is linear-in-pixels below 512x512 and only becomes superlinear going UP (attention is quadratic in tokens; at 1080p the first attention level carries ~63x the work against 7.9x for convolution). There is a hard floor around 3-4 ms per step from dispatch overhead and SM underutilization. The lever pays between 512x512 and 1080p and is nearly exhausted below it. Do not plan around shrinking to 360p.

SECOND CORRECTION, and it is the one that decides everything: every diffusion number in the literature is uncontended, and the contention behaviour of a 10 ms dispatch on this 4090 is UNMEASURED. The five measured contention points fit an additive model (+2 to +7 ms, one lost scheduling quantum) exactly as well as a multiplicative one (5.95-13x), because they are all under 1.5 ms idle. ### Legal shape of shipping a prompt-driven stylizer that names copyrighted properties — PhyriadFG, one-person project, single self-contained .exe, no model training. Scope: US + EU, current to 2026-09-07

- **Artistic STYLE is not protected by copyright in the US. Specific characters and specific works are. This is the whole answer to the first question, and it favours the operator.**
  - US Copyright Office, plain language: copyright "is not affected by style or form" — style sits on the idea side of the idea/expression dichotomy, which is codified at 17 U.S.C. §102(b). Concretely: flat cel shading, thick black outlines, four-fingered hands, yellow skin, rubber-hose limbs, overbite mouths are unprotectable elements. Bart Simpson as a character, and any individual frame of the show, are protected. NOT SETTLED at the edges: a 2025 law-review survey finds federal courts "equally split" on whether style ever crosses into protected expression, all agreeing the answer lies in the idea/expression distinction. Treat "style is free" as correct in the main and contested at the boundary.
  - source: https://www.copyright.gov/engage/docs/visual_art.pdf ; https://scholarship.law.duke.edu/faculty_scholarship/4522 ; https://itsartlaw.org/art-law/recent-developments-in-ai-art-copyright-copyright-office-report-new-registrations/
- **The EU reaches the same result by a different route, and arguably a cleaner one: a style cannot be a "work" at all under EU law.**
  - CJEU Levola Hengelo (C-310/17) and Cofemel (C-683/17): for subject matter to be a 'work' it must be "expressed in a manner which makes it identifiable with sufficient precision and objectivity". Cofemel expressly holds that an "aesthetic effect" is "the result of subjective sensation of beauty felt by each person who looks at it" and therefore fails that precision-and-objectivity test. A style IS an aesthetic effect. So no EU copyright subsists in style as such. Note the EU has no fair-use defence, so the US/EU gap matters for TRAINING and for character reproduction — not for the style question, where both jurisdictions land in the same place.
  - source: https://ipkitten.blogspot.com/2019/09/the-cofemel-decision-well-beyond-simple.html ; https://www.citma.org.uk/resources/the-cofemel-decision.html
- **DECISIVE, AND SIX MONTHS OLD: the Supreme Court answered the user-prompt-vs-developer-preset question directly in Cox v. Sony, and it narrowed developer liability. A general prompt box is now clearly on the safe side.**
  - Cox Communications, Inc. v. Sony Music Entertainment, No. 24-171, 607 U.S. ___ (2026), decided 2026-03-25, Thomas J. (Roberts, Alito, Kagan, Gorsuch, Kavanaugh, Barrett; Sotomayor + Jackson concurring in judgment). Verbatim holding: "The provider of a service is contributorily liable for a user's infringement only if it intended that the provided service be used for infringement, which can be shown only if the party induced the infringement or the provided service is tailored to that infringement." Two prongs, and only two. Inducement: "A provider induces infringement if it actively encourages infringement through specific acts, as in Grokster, where file-sharing software companies promoted and marketed their software as a tool to infringe copyrights." Tailored: "A service is tailored to infringement if it is 'not capable of substantial or commercially significant noninfringing uses.'" And the load-bearing sentence for a tool author: "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient to establish the required intent to infringe." The Court vacated a $1B judgment on this basis. VERIFIED against the slip opinion PDF, not a secondary summary.
  - source: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-171_new_3dq3.pdf
- **Applying Cox to the operator's exact question: a free-text prompt box is user conduct; a shipped preset button labelled "Simpsons" is developer conduct, and it is the Grokster fact pattern almost word for word.**
  - A prompt box in a frame-generation overlay is "capable of substantial noninfringing uses" by an enormous margin (photoreal relight, painterly, desaturation, cel shading, colour grading — none of it touching anyone's IP), so the 'tailored' prong fails outright. And the developer performs no "specific act" of encouragement — he ships an empty text field. A preset labelled with a property inverts both facts: it is the developer, not the user, choosing to name it, and it is precisely "promoted and marketed... as a tool to" produce that property. Cox also credits Cox's mitigation — it "repeatedly discouraged copyright infringement" — which is a cheap, concrete thing a solo developer can replicate in a README line. CONFIDENCE: high on the doctrine, medium on the application — no court has yet held that a named style preset is inducement. This is a strong analogy, not a holding.
  - source: https://www.supremecourt.gov/opinions/25pdf/24-171_new_3dq3.pdf ; https://natlawreview.com/article/copy-secondary-liability-age-ai

So the working rule the largest vendor follows is: describe the mechanism, or name a source only if it is out of copyright. ReShade preset communities land in the same place by instinct: descriptive or author-branded names ("Sunset Filter", "Natural", "Wuffi's ReShade Preset", "GJG's ReShade Preset").

- **What shipped products actually do, part 2 — even the vendor that markets itself as "commercially safe" refuses to stand behind a prompt that names a property, and OpenAI's much-publicised line is a policy choice, not a legal one.**
  - Adobe's commercial indemnity for Firefly output does NOT cover output generated from prompts that referenced a third party's trademark or a named artist. So Adobe's own position is: our generic presets are ours to warrant, YOUR prompt naming a property is yours to own. That is precisely the split the operator should build into his product. Separately, OpenAI (2025 4o image system card and follow-up statements): "We continue to prevent generations in the style of individual living artists, but we do permit broader studio styles." This drew heavy criticism because Miyazaki is alive, and the artist/studio distinction has no basis in copyright doctrine. USE IT AS EVIDENCE OF WHERE A HEAVILY-LAWYERED VENDOR PARKED ITS RISK, NOT AS A RULE — a solo dev copying OpenAI's line would be copying a PR compromise.

- **Nominative fair use lets him name GAMES freely and does not let him name STYLES. The distinction is mechanical and easy to apply.**
  - Nominative fair use requires: (1) the thing is not readily identifiable without the mark, (2) only so much of the mark as is necessary is used, (3) nothing suggests sponsorship or endorsement. "Preset tuned for Battlefield 6" satisfies all three — there is no other way to say which game it is compatible with, and compatibility statements are the textbook case. "Simpsons preset" satisfies none of them — the style IS readily describable without the mark (flat cel, yellow, heavy outline), so prong 1 fails immediately, and the mark is being used for its pull, not for identification. Practical upshot: PhyriadFG can name every game it supports. It cannot name a style after a property.
  - source: https://www.inta.org/fact-sheets/fair-use-of-trademarks-intended-for-a-non-legal-audience/ ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominative_use
- **HOW a preset is BUILT matters more than what it is called. A text-string preset is a string literal. An image-conditioned preset means shipping copies of copyrighted frames — that is DIRECT infringement, a different and much worse category than inducement.**
  - Given the constraint that the operator cannot train, the only ways to build a 'Simpsons preset' are (a) a text string fed to the model, or (b) a reference image / IP-Adapter conditioning image / embedding derived from frames. Route (b) puts actual copies of Fox-Disney frames inside the distributed .exe. That is straightforward reproduction and distribution under 17 U.S.C. §106(1) and (3) — no secondary-liability analysis needed, no Cox protection, no style defence, because the style defence protects the OUTPUT's look, not the INPUT's provenance. This is the trap most likely to catch a hobbyist who reasons "I'll just bundle one reference picture." CONFIDENCE high on the doctrine; this is basic reproduction law, not an AI question.
  - source: https://www.copyright.gov/title17/

  - The NOTICE-file, ship-a-copy-of-the-licence and pass-the-restrictions-downstream obligations in both OpenRAIL (§4) and the Stability Community License are triggered by DISTRIBUTING the model or a derivative. If PhyriadFG ships as a self-contained .exe that fetches the weights from the licensor's own host on first launch, the user obtains the model directly from the licensor under the licensor's terms, and the developer distributes only his own code. The remaining obligations are the ones attached to USE (the AUP, where applicable) rather than to redistribution. CONFIDENCE medium — this is the standard reading and standard industry practice, but it is my reading of the licence text, not a clause that says so, and it does not help at all with FLUX.1 [dev], where the bar is non-commercial USE, not distribution.

**Recommendation:** SHORT VERSION: style is free, names are not, and the Supreme Court moved the line in his favour six months ago — but only if he does not ship the names himself.

Three practices, each cheap, none of which turns this into a legal project.

1) SHIP A PROMPT BOX, SHIP ZERO PRESETS NAMED AFTER A PROPERTY. This is the single decision that matters and it is binary. Under Cox v. Sony (2026-03-25) a tool with substantial noninfringing uses whose developer performs no "specific act" of encouragement is not contributorily liable, and "mere knowledge that a service will be used to infringe is insufficient." An empty text field is exactly that. A button labelled "Simpsons" is the developer choosing to write the word, and it is the Grokster fact pattern — promoting the tool as a way to get that property. The user typing "estilo simpsons" is user conduct and stays user conduct. He still gets presets, and should — just name them by MECHANISM, the way Firefly names all ~130 of its presets and names zero after anyone: "Cel Shaded / Flat Ink", "Rubber Hose Cartoon", "Halftone Comic", "Photoreal Relight", "Posterized". Public-domain sources may be named outright, which is what Photoshop does with Van Gogh and Monet. And extend the rule to the marketing surface — a demo video titled "PhyriadFG Simpsons mode" is the same inducement fact as the preset; Grokster looked at "express promotion, marketing, and intent to promote". Naming the GAMES he supports is fine and always was: that is nominative fair use.

2) TWO SENTENCES IN THE README, AND NEVER BUNDLE A REFERENCE IMAGE. The README line: "PhyriadFG generates images from text you supply. You are responsible for what you prompt and for any use of the result. Do not use it to reproduce characters or works you do not own or have rights to." That is the whole compliance posture, and Cox expressly credited the defendant for having "repeatedly discouraged copyright infringement". The harder rule is the second half: a text-string preset is a string literal, but an image-conditioned preset (reference image, IP-Adapter conditioning, an embedding derived from frames) puts actual copies of studio frames inside the .exe. That is direct infringement under §106 — no style defence, no Cox protection, a different and much worse category. Since he cannot train anyway, this reduces to one flat rule: no copyrighted frames in the distribution, in any form.
